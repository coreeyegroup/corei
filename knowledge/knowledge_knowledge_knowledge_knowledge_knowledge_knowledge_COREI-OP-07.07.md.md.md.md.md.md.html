1. PURPOSE

The COREI Trading Operations document defines the
authoritative institutional operating model for
running,
controlling,
observing,
and recovering the complete trading lifecycle.

Its purpose is to ensure that trading activity
remains

authorized,

deterministic,

risk-controlled,

portfolio-aware,

broker-aware,

account-aware,

market-aware,

observable,

reconcilable,

recoverable,

and institutionally accountable.

Trading Operations SHALL preserve the complete
authority chain from market observation to final
financial state.

2. SCOPE

This document governs operational trading across

Forex,

Commodities,

Crypto,

Equities,

Indices,

Derivatives,

Options,

Futures,

and future approved asset classes.

It governs

market intelligence,

signal intelligence,

decision intelligence,

risk intelligence,

portfolio intelligence,

execution intelligence,

position intelligence,

performance intelligence,

system intelligence,

accounts,

brokers,

strategies,

portfolios,

assets,

markets,

regions,

currencies,

custodians,

exchanges,

jurisdictions,

operators,

automation,

and artificial intelligence systems.

market understanding,

decision correctness,

risk control,

portfolio integrity,

execution safety,

position accuracy,

financial state,

or trading continuity.

Trading incidents SHALL be classified according to

86. TRADING INCIDENT TYPES

stale market data,

incorrect signal,

decision defect,

risk-control failure,

portfolio-control failure,

duplicate order,

unknown order state,

87. TRADING INCIDENT TRIAGE

what trading capability is affected,

which accounts are affected,

which brokers are affected,

which strategies are affected,

which portfolios are affected,

which instruments are affected,

whether financial exposure exists,

and whether new trading must be suspended.

disable strategy,

disable account,

disable broker,

block new orders,

cancel open orders,

enter close-only mode,

close positions,

isolate data source,

or activate fallback.

Containment SHALL prioritize capital protection and

signals,

decisions,

risk,

portfolio state,

orders,

executions,

positions,

balances,

broker state,

and system health

Recovery SHALL establish

authoritative order state,

authoritative execution state,

reconciled position state,

reconciled account state,

valid risk state,

valid portfolio state,

and safe trading readiness.

detection,

affected accounts,

affected orders,

unknown state handling,

alternate broker policy,

position management,

recovery,

and reconciliation.

92. MARKET DATA OUTAGE

affected instruments,

affected strategies,

fallback providers,

staleness thresholds,

trading restrictions,

and recovery validation.

Stale data SHALL not silently continue to drive
trading.

93. RISK SYSTEM OUTAGE

safe fallback exists.

Risk-system unavailability SHALL not be treated as
risk approval.

94. POSITION STATE UNCERTAINTY

Unknown or conflicting position state SHALL trigger
controlled restriction.

New trading SHOULD be blocked where position
uncertainty could create unsafe exposure.

Reconciliation SHALL precede normal resumption.

95. DUPLICATE ORDER INCIDENT

order identification,

broker verification,

exposure assessment,

containment,

and reconciliation.

Duplicate execution risk SHALL be treated as
financially consequential.

96. RUNAWAY TRADING

Runaway trading SHALL include uncontrolled

order generation,

order repetition,

position accumulation,

or risk expansion.

Controls SHALL detect and contain runaway behaviour
through

limits,

rate controls,

exposure controls,

kill switches,

and operational monitoring.

97. TRADING CONTINUITY

Critical trading capability SHALL define continuity

Continuity SHALL identify

minimum safe capability,

required data,

required risk controls,

required broker access,

required position authority,

alternate providers,

alternate brokers where approved,

and decision authority.

infrastructure,

security,

data,

platforms,

services,

risk,

orders,

positions,

and balances.

100. TRADING RECONCILIATION

Reconciliation is a mandatory institutional
control.

Trading Operations SHALL reconcile where
applicable

orders,

executions,

positions,

balances,

margin,

fees,

and financial outcomes.

Unknown discrepancy SHALL remain visible.

101. RECONCILIATION FREQUENCY

Reconciliation frequency SHALL reflect

asset class,

market,

broker,

account,

strategy,

and risk.

Reconciliation MAY be

event-driven,

continuous,

intraday,

end-of-session,

or scheduled.

Critical state SHALL not wait unnecessarily for
batch reconciliation.

102. RECONCILIATION BREAK

A reconciliation break SHALL preserve

identity,

scope,

internal value,

external value,

difference,

time,

impact,

and owner.

Material unresolved breaks SHALL restrict trading
where required.

103. TRADING OBSERVABILITY

Trading Operations SHALL provide end-to-end
observability.

Observability SHALL include

market state,

signal state,

decision state,

risk state,

portfolio state,

order state,

execution state,

position state,

performance state,

broker state,

and system state.

104. TRADING METRICS

Trading metrics MAY include

signals generated,

decisions approved,

decisions rejected,

risk rejections,

orders submitted,

orders rejected,

fill rate,

partial fills,

execution latency,

slippage,

position exposure,

PnL,

drawdown,

reconciliation breaks,

and broker health.

Metrics SHALL support operational decisions.

105. TRADING ALERTING

Critical trading conditions SHALL generate
actionable alerts.

Alerts MAY include

stale market data,

risk rejection spike,

broker disconnect,

order rejection,

unknown order state,

duplicate order suspicion,

position mismatch,

margin pressure,

drawdown breach,

or reconciliation failure.

Alerts SHALL have ownership.

106. TRADING DASHBOARDS

Trading dashboards SHALL present actionable
institutional state.

Dashboards SHOULD expose

market health,

strategy state,

risk state,

portfolio exposure,

orders,

executions,

positions,

performance,

broker health,

and incidents.

Visual availability SHALL not replace underlying
control.

107. TRADING AUDIT

Consequential trading actions SHALL preserve audit
evidence.

Audit MAY include

strategy enablement,

decision,

risk approval,

risk rejection,

portfolio approval,

order authorization,

order submission,

manual intervention,

suspension,

resume,

and emergency action.

108. TRADING EVIDENCE

Trading evidence SHALL support reconstruction of

what the market state was,

what signal existed,

what decision was formed,

what risk evaluation occurred,

what portfolio evaluation occurred,

what order was authorized,

what the broker reported,

what execution occurred,

what position resulted,

and what financial outcome followed.

109. TRADING RUNBOOKS

Critical trading scenarios SHALL maintain usable
runbooks.

Runbooks SHOULD cover

market data outage,

broker outage,

risk outage,

unknown order state,

duplicate order,

position mismatch,

margin issue,

runaway trading,

emergency stop,

and recovery.

Runbooks SHALL reflect actual trading architecture.

110. TRADING ON-CALL

Critical trading capability SHALL define an
appropriate operational and escalation model.

The model SHALL identify

trading operations,

strategy owner,

risk authority,

portfolio authority,

execution owner,

broker escalation,

infrastructure escalation,

security escalation,

and executive escalation

where applicable.

111. TRADING HANDOVER

Responsibility transfer SHALL preserve

active positions,

open orders,

unknown orders,

broker issues,

market issues,

risk restrictions,

reconciliation breaks,

temporary controls,

and required follow-up.

Critical financial context SHALL not be lost.

112. TRADING CHANGE

Material trading change SHALL define

owner,

scope,

affected strategies,

affected accounts,

affected brokers,

affected portfolios,

affected instruments,

risk impact,

execution impact,

validation,

and recovery.

Trading change SHALL receive control proportional
to financial impact.

113. STRATEGY CHANGE

Production strategy change SHALL preserve

strategy identity,

version,

logic change,

data dependencies,

risk impact,

portfolio impact,

validation,

and activation authority.

Code deployment SHALL not automatically authorize
strategy activation.

114. RISK LIMIT CHANGE

Risk limit changes SHALL be

authorized,

versioned,

effective-time controlled,

observable,

and auditable.

Limit increase SHALL receive appropriate authority.

115. BROKER CONFIGURATION CHANGE

Broker configuration change SHALL consider

accounts,

credentials,

endpoints,

instrument mapping,

order semantics,

rate limits,

and execution risk.

Changes SHALL be validated before normal trading.

116. TRADING CONFIGURATION

Production trading configuration SHALL be

controlled,

versioned,

reviewable,

validated,

and attributable.

Hidden production trading configuration is
prohibited.

117. TRADING CONFIGURATION DRIFT

Trading configuration drift SHALL be detectable.

Drift MAY affect

strategy parameters,

risk limits,

portfolio allocations,

broker routing,

account enablement,

or execution policy.

Unexplained drift SHALL be investigated.

118. TRADING TESTING

Trading capability SHALL be tested according to
risk.

Testing MAY include

unit tests,

integration tests,

historical backtests,

simulation,

paper trading,

broker sandbox testing,

failure testing,

reconciliation testing,

and recovery testing.

Backtest success SHALL NOT alone prove production
readiness.

119. PAPER TRADING VALIDATION

Paper trading SHALL validate production-like
behaviour without live financial execution.

Validation SHOULD include

market data,

signals,

decisions,

risk,

portfolio,

orders,

execution simulation,

positions,

performance,

observability,

and recovery.

Paper mode SHALL remain isolated from live
authority.

reconciliation,

and emergency controls.

121. TRADING DEBT

Trading operational debt SHALL be visible.

Debt MAY include

manual reconciliation,

unknown order states,

weak broker abstraction,

missing risk aggregation,

incomplete observability,

uncontrolled configuration,

or untested recovery.

Trading debt MAY create direct financial risk.

122. TRADING EXCEPTION

Trading operational exceptions SHALL be governed.

Every exception SHALL define

requirement,

reason,

scope,

affected accounts,

affected strategies,

affected portfolios,

financial risk,

owner,

compensating controls,

monitoring,

and expiration.

Permanent undocumented trading exceptions are
prohibited.

123. NO TRADING PATCH WORK

Trading Operations SHALL NOT permanently compensate
for structural defects through

manual order correction,

hidden strategy logic,

spreadsheet-only state,

operator memory,

duplicate execution paths,

or uncontrolled broker workarounds.

The defect SHALL be corrected at its proper
ownership layer.

124. NO DIRECT SIGNAL-TO-ORDER BYPASS

A signal SHALL NOT directly create a live order
without required

decision,

risk,

portfolio,

execution authorization,

and broker controls.

Production pressure SHALL not bypass the
institutional chain.

125. NO RISK BYPASS

Mandatory risk controls SHALL NOT be bypassed by

strategy,

operator,

service,

automation,

AI,

or infrastructure access

without explicit governed emergency authority.

Risk unavailability SHALL not equal risk approval.

126. NO BLIND ORDER RETRY

Unknown order submission state SHALL NOT trigger
blind retry.

Authoritative broker state SHALL be determined
before retry where duplicate execution is possible.

127. NO INTERNAL-STATE ASSUMPTION

Internal state SHALL NOT automatically override
external financial authority.

Broker,
exchange,
or custodian state

SHALL be reconciled where applicable.

128. NO SILENT POSITION UNCERTAINTY

Unknown or conflicting position state SHALL remain
explicit.

Normal trading SHALL be restricted where position
uncertainty creates unacceptable risk.

129. NO TECHNICAL AUTHORITY COLLAPSE

Technical access SHALL NOT automatically create

strategy authority,

risk authority,

portfolio authority,

execution authority,

or capital authority.

Institutional authority boundaries SHALL remain
explicit.

130. NO AI AUTHORITY EXPANSION

Artificial intelligence SHALL NOT expand its own
trading authority.

AI MAY support intelligence.

Deterministic institutional controls SHALL govern
consequential financial action.

131. NO FALSE TRADING HEALTH

Trading SHALL not be declared healthy solely
because

services are running,

broker sessions are connected,

or market data is arriving.

Health SHALL reflect safe end-to-end trading
capability.

135. STRATEGY DEPRECATION

Deprecated strategies SHALL define

affected portfolios,

open positions,

open orders,

capital,

replacement or closure plan,

and retirement condition.

Deprecation SHALL be visible.

136. STRATEGY RETIREMENT

A strategy SHALL be retired only after

new trading is disabled,

open orders are resolved,

positions are resolved or transferred,

capital is released,

performance is preserved,

configuration is archived,

and documentation is updated.

Hidden active exposure SHALL block retirement.

137. RELATIONSHIP TO PRODUCTION OPERATIONS

Production Operations defines the common
institutional production model.

Trading Operations extends that model for
consequential financial capability.

Every production trading capability SHALL comply
with both.

138. RELATIONSHIP TO PLATFORM OPERATIONS

Platforms provide shared institutional capability.

Trading Operations consumes platform capability
without transferring trading authority to the
platform layer.

Platform failure SHALL be reflected in trading
readiness.

139. RELATIONSHIP TO SERVICE OPERATIONS

Services implement trading capabilities.

Service Operations owns service health and runtime.

Trading Operations owns the financial authority
chain and operational trading outcome.

Service availability SHALL not alone define trading
readiness.

140. RELATIONSHIP TO INFRASTRUCTURE OPERATIONS

Infrastructure Operations owns technical
foundations.

Trading Operations owns safe use of those
foundations for financial activity.

Infrastructure access SHALL not create trading
authority.

141. RELATIONSHIP TO DATA OPERATIONS

Data Operations preserves

authority,

quality,

freshness,

lineage,

and recoverability

of trading data.

Trading Operations consumes data according to
readiness requirements.

Data availability SHALL not automatically mean
trading readiness.

142. RELATIONSHIP TO SECURITY OPERATIONS

Security Operations protects

identity,

access,

secrets,

broker credentials,

data,

infrastructure,

and trading authority.

Security administration SHALL not create trading
authority.

Trading continuity SHALL not override mandatory
security controls.
