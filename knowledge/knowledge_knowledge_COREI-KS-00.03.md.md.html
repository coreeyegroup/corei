COREI KNOWLEDGE ARCHITECTURE

KNOWLEDGE ARCHITECTURE

VERSION

1.0

STATUS

APPROVED

CLASSIFICATION

CONSTITUTIONAL

1. PURPOSE

The COREI Knowledge Architecture defines the structural
organization of institutional knowledge.

Its purpose is to establish a deterministic architecture
through which Knowledge Assets are organized,
related, inherited, governed, navigated,
and continuously evolved.

The Knowledge Architecture exists independently
of any implementation technology.

The repository is one implementation.

The architecture is permanent.

2. ARCHITECTURAL OBJECTIVES

The Knowledge Architecture exists to

• organize institutional knowledge

• define ownership

• establish inheritance

• eliminate ambiguity

• support AI-native navigation

• support deterministic retrieval

• enable institutional scalability

• preserve long-term continuity

3. ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLES

The COREI Knowledge Architecture follows these
constitutional principles.

Knowledge has one authoritative owner.

Knowledge has one authoritative location.

Knowledge shall be organized by responsibility.

Knowledge shall inherit from higher authority.

Knowledge shall reference rather than duplicate.

Knowledge shall remain deterministic.

Knowledge shall remain machine-readable.

Knowledge shall remain institutionally scalable.

4. KNOWLEDGE LAYERS

Institutional knowledge is organized into
four architectural layers.

Knowledge Foundation

↓

Knowledge Domains

↓

Knowledge Assets

↓

Knowledge Content

Each layer provides structure for the layer below.

Authority flows downward.

Dependencies may flow upward.

Knowledge never bypasses architectural layers.

5. KNOWLEDGE FOUNDATION

The Knowledge Foundation governs the
entire Knowledge System.

It defines

Knowledge Model

Knowledge System

Knowledge Architecture

Knowledge Governance

Knowledge Lifecycle

Knowledge Classification

Knowledge Asset Standard

Knowledge Naming Standard

Knowledge Repository Standard

Every Knowledge Domain inherits
from the Knowledge Foundation.

6. KNOWLEDGE DOMAINS

Knowledge Domains partition institutional knowledge
according to responsibility.

Each domain owns exactly one institutional concern.

Knowledge Domains shall not overlap.

Knowledge Domains inherit from the
Knowledge Foundation.

Knowledge Assets inherit from
their owning Knowledge Domain.

7. KNOWLEDGE ASSETS

Knowledge Assets represent the authoritative
units of institutional knowledge.

Every Knowledge Asset shall

have one identifier,

have one owner,

have one responsibility,

have one authoritative location,

define explicit dependencies,

maintain version history,

support institutional traceability.

8. KNOWLEDGE RELATIONSHIPS

Knowledge Assets interact through
explicit architectural relationships.

Supported relationships include

Inheritance

Reference

Dependency

Extension

Constraint

Governance

Support

Relationships shall remain explicit.

Hidden dependencies are prohibited.

Knowledge duplication is prohibited.

9. ARCHITECTURAL INHERITANCE

Institutional authority follows one direction.

Knowledge Foundation

↓

Knowledge Domain

↓

Knowledge Asset

↓

Knowledge Content

Every lower layer inherits
the constitutional rules
defined above it.

Lower layers may strengthen
higher-layer principles.

They shall never contradict them.

10. KNOWLEDGE NAVIGATION

The Knowledge Architecture shall support
deterministic navigation.

Knowledge shall be discoverable by

Repository Path

Knowledge Domain

Knowledge Asset Identifier

Classification

Owner

Relationships

Dependencies

Keywords

Canonical Vocabulary

Artificial intelligence and human users
shall navigate the same architecture.

11. ARCHITECTURAL SCALABILITY

The Knowledge Architecture is designed to
support institutional growth over decades.

It shall support

thousands of Knowledge Assets,

multiple repositories,

multiple engineering teams,

multiple AI systems,

multiple operational environments,

future COREI platforms,

and future institutional domains.

Growth shall not require
architectural redesign.

12. AUTHORITY

The COREI Knowledge Architecture defines the
constitutional structure of institutional knowledge.

Every Knowledge Foundation document,

Knowledge Domain,

Knowledge Asset,

engineering standard,

AI system,

automation workflow,

platform,

and operational capability

shall conform to this architecture.

Changes to this architecture require
constitutional review.

The architecture is expected to remain
stable throughout the lifetime of COREI.
