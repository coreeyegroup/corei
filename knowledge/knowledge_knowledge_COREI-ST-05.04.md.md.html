COREI ENGINEERING STANDARDS

DOCUMENT ID

COREI-ST-05.04

DOCUMENT NAME

ENGINEERING STANDARDS

VERSION

1.0

STATUS

APPROVED

CLASSIFICATION

CONSTITUTIONAL

1. PURPOSE

The COREI Engineering Standards define the
constitutional engineering standards governing
every engineering activity performed within the
COREI Institution.

Their purpose is to ensure that engineering
remains consistent, deterministic, measurable,
maintainable, secure, observable, and fully
aligned with constitutional Identity,
Governance, Architecture, Engineering,
and Institutional Standards.

Engineering Standards establish one engineering
language across the entire institution.

2. ENGINEERING PHILOSOPHY

Within COREI,

engineering standards do not define technology.

Engineering standards define engineering quality.

Every engineer shall produce implementations
that exhibit consistent architectural fidelity,
engineering discipline, operational reliability,
and institutional maintainability regardless of
technology stack or engineering team.

Engineering consistency is an institutional asset.

Engineering excellence shall be repeatable.

3. CONSTITUTIONAL ENGINEERING STANDARDS

Every engineering activity shall comply with the
following constitutional standards.

Standard 1

Architecture Before Implementation.

Implementation shall never precede approved
architecture.

Standard 2

Describe → Implement → Verify → Validate →
Document.

Every engineering activity shall follow the
constitutional execution lifecycle.

Standard 3

No Patch Work.

Engineering shall permanently resolve root causes.

Temporary fixes shall never become institutional
solutions.

Standard 4

Deterministic Engineering.

Every implementation shall produce predictable,
repeatable, and reproducible outcomes.

Standard 5

Explicit Ownership.

Every engineering artifact shall have clearly
defined ownership and responsibility.

Standard 6

Contract First.

Interfaces, APIs, schemas, events, and service
contracts shall be defined before implementation.

Standard 7

Observability By Default.

Every implementation shall expose sufficient
logging, metrics, tracing, health information,
and audit evidence.

Standard 8

Security By Design.

Security shall be engineered into every solution
from its inception.

Standard 9

Production Readiness.

Engineering shall produce production-grade
capabilities rather than prototype-quality
implementations.

Standard 10

Knowledge Preservation.

Every engineering activity shall increase the
institutional engineering knowledge base.

4. ENGINEERING STANDARD CATEGORIES

Engineering Standards govern

implementation standards,

repository standards,

coding standards,

architecture compliance,

API standards,

event standards,

integration standards,

testing standards,

deployment standards,

quality standards,

documentation standards,

automation standards,

DevSecOps standards,

Site Reliability standards,

and future engineering standards.

All engineering standards inherit from this
constitutional document.

5. ENGINEERING RESPONSIBILITIES

Engineering Standards preserve

implementation consistency,

engineering quality,

architectural fidelity,

repository integrity,

contract consistency,

production readiness,

operational reliability,

engineering maintainability,

knowledge continuity,

and long-term institutional engineering maturity.

Engineering Standards guide engineering
implementation without replacing engineering
judgement.

6. ENGINEERING COMPLIANCE

Every engineering implementation shall demonstrate
compliance with

constitutional architecture,

engineering principles,

institutional standards,

approved specifications,

security requirements,

quality requirements,

validation requirements,

production readiness,

and documentation requirements.

Engineering compliance shall be measurable and
auditable.

7. ENGINEERING VALIDATION

Every engineering implementation shall answer the
following constitutional questions.

Has approved architecture been implemented?

Has every engineering standard been satisfied?

Are interfaces contract-compliant?

Is ownership explicit?

Is implementation deterministic?

Has verification completed?

Has validation completed?

Is production readiness demonstrated?

Has documentation been completed?

Has institutional engineering knowledge increased?

If any answer is negative,

engineering compliance remains incomplete.

8. ENGINEERING DECLARATION

COREI Engineering Standards exist to ensure that
engineering remains one institutional capability
rather than a collection of independent technical
practices.

Engineering Standards preserve

engineering consistency,

implementation quality,

architectural integrity,

operational excellence,

institutional maintainability,

knowledge continuity,

and constitutional engineering maturity.

Engineering Standards shall continuously mature
through institutional learning.

9. AUTHORITY

This document defines the constitutional
Engineering Standards of COREI.

Every engineering team,

repository,

microservice,

platform,

workflow,

automation,

API,

runtime,

deployment,

engineering guide,

implementation framework,

and future engineering capability

shall comply with these Engineering Standards.

Changes to this document require constitutional
review.
