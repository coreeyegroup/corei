COREI DECISION GOVERNANCE

DOCUMENT ID

COREI-GV-02.04

DOCUMENT NAME

DECISION GOVERNANCE

VERSION

1.0

STATUS

APPROVED

CLASSIFICATION

CONSTITUTIONAL

1. PURPOSE

The COREI Decision Governance defines the
constitutional framework through which every
institutional decision is evaluated,
authorized,
executed,
validated,
and continuously improved.

Its purpose is to ensure that every decision
strengthens constitutional identity,
institutional intelligence,
and long-term institutional capability.

2. DECISION PHILOSOPHY

Within COREI,

decisions are not opinions.

Decisions are governed institutional actions
derived from validated understanding.

Every decision shall be supported by

knowledge,

understanding,

institutional intelligence,

constitutional governance,

and explicit accountability.

Authority alone shall never justify a decision.

3. DECISION LIFECYCLE

Every institutional decision shall follow the
same deterministic lifecycle.

Observe Reality

↓

Acquire Knowledge

↓

Develop Understanding

↓

Generate Institutional Intelligence

↓

Evaluate Alternatives

↓

Assess Risk

↓

Authorize Decision

↓

Execute Decision

↓

Measure Outcome

↓

Capture Learning

↓

Continuously Improve

No constitutional decision shall bypass this lifecycle.

4. DECISION PRINCIPLES

Every decision shall satisfy the following
constitutional principles.

Identity Alignment

Every decision shall preserve COREI Identity.

Knowledge Validation

Every decision shall be supported by validated
institutional knowledge.

Evidence-Based Reasoning

Evidence shall outweigh assumptions,
preferences,
and hierarchy.

Explainability

Every significant decision shall be explainable
and traceable.

Responsible Authority

Only authorized institutional authority may
approve constitutional decisions.

Accountability

Every decision shall have one accountable owner.

Long-Term Stewardship

Long-term institutional capability shall take
precedence over short-term optimization.

5. DECISION CATEGORIES

Institutional decisions are classified into
five constitutional categories.

Constitutional Decisions

Identity,
Mission,
Philosophy,
DNA,
Knowledge Foundation.

Strategic Decisions

Institutional direction,
investments,
major initiatives,
organizational evolution.

Architectural Decisions

System architecture,
platform architecture,
technology architecture,
knowledge architecture.

Operational Decisions

Execution,
operations,
deployment,
support,
runtime activities.

Adaptive Decisions

Continuous improvement,
optimization,
learning,
experimentation,
innovation.

Each category follows the same governance model
while operating at different institutional levels.

6. DECISION OWNERSHIP

Every decision shall define

one accountable owner,

decision authority,

decision rationale,

supporting evidence,

expected outcome,

review criteria,

and institutional impact.

Shared accountability shall not replace
explicit ownership.

7. DECISION VALIDATION

Before approval,
every significant decision shall answer
the following questions.

Does this preserve COREI Identity?

Does it strengthen institutional understanding?

Is the supporting knowledge authoritative?

Is the reasoning explainable?

Have risks been evaluated?

Is authority legitimate?

Is accountability explicit?

Does this strengthen long-term institutional capability?

If any answer is negative,

the decision shall be reviewed before approval.

8. DECISION LEARNING

Every decision shall produce institutional learning.

Learning shall include

decision quality,

execution quality,

observed outcomes,

unexpected outcomes,

root causes,

knowledge improvements,

governance improvements,

and institutional recommendations.

No significant decision shall conclude
without institutional learning.

9. DECISION DECLARATION

COREI governs every institutional decision
through disciplined understanding,
validated knowledge,
responsible authority,
explicit accountability,
and continuous learning.

Institutional decisions shall strengthen
knowledge before action,
understanding before authority,
and long-term institutional mastery before
short-term optimization.

10. AUTHORITY

This document defines the constitutional
Decision Governance model of COREI.

Every Governance Framework,

Authority Model,

Engineering Decision,

Architecture Decision,

Artificial Intelligence Decision,

Operational Decision,

Research Decision,

and future institutional decision

shall comply with this governance model.

Changes to this document require
constitutional review.
