COREI RISK GOVERNANCE

DOCUMENT ID

COREI-GV-02.07

DOCUMENT NAME

RISK GOVERNANCE

VERSION

1.0

STATUS

APPROVED

CLASSIFICATION

CONSTITUTIONAL

1. PURPOSE

The COREI Risk Governance defines the constitutional
principles through which institutional uncertainty,
exposure, resilience, and continuity are governed.

Its purpose is to ensure that every institutional
capability manages uncertainty without compromising
COREI’s constitutional identity, institutional
integrity, or long-term sustainability.

2. RISK PHILOSOPHY

Within COREI,

risk is not the enemy.

Unknown risk is.

Risk is the uncertainty that accompanies
institutional decisions.

Understanding reduces uncertainty.

Governance ensures uncertainty remains within
acceptable institutional boundaries.

Risk governance therefore exists to preserve
institutional continuity rather than eliminate risk.

3. RISK GOVERNANCE PRINCIPLES

Institutional risk governance shall follow these
constitutional principles.

Identity Before Opportunity.

Constitutional integrity shall never be sacrificed
for short-term advantage.

Understanding Before Exposure.

Institutional understanding shall precede
institutional commitment.

Evidence Before Acceptance.

Risk shall be evaluated using authoritative
knowledge and evidence.

Transparency Before Escalation.

Material risks shall remain visible,
traceable,
and explainable.

Resilience Before Optimization.

Long-term institutional resilience takes
precedence over short-term efficiency.

Learning Before Repetition.

Every realized risk shall strengthen future
institutional governance.

4. RISK GOVERNANCE MODEL

Institutional risk governance follows a
continuous constitutional lifecycle.

Observe

↓

Identify

↓

Understand

↓

Evaluate

↓

Govern

↓

Authorize

↓

Monitor

↓

Learn

↓

Strengthen Governance

This lifecycle applies to every
institutional capability.

5. RISK RESPONSIBILITIES

Risk Governance owns

risk governance principles,

risk acceptance authority,

risk oversight,

institutional resilience,

governance escalation,

and constitutional protection.

Risk Governance does not own

risk calculations,

technical controls,

operational procedures,

engineering safeguards,

or implementation mechanisms.

Those belong to their respective domains.

6. GOVERNANCE CONSTRAINTS

No institutional capability shall

accept unknown material risk,

ignore constitutional impact,

bypass governance review,

conceal institutional exposure,

weaken institutional resilience,

or compromise long-term continuity.

Institutional risk shall remain visible
to governance.

7. RISK VALIDATION

Before accepting significant institutional risk,
the following questions shall be answered.

Has the risk been identified?

Is the risk understood?

Has the constitutional impact been evaluated?

Is the supporting evidence sufficient?

Is the accepting authority legitimate?

Can the institution recover?

Will institutional continuity remain protected?

If any answer is negative,

the risk shall not be accepted.

8. RISK DECLARATION

COREI governs institutional risk through
understanding,
constitutional discipline,
responsible authority,
continuous oversight,
and institutional learning.

The objective of Risk Governance is not
to eliminate uncertainty.

Its objective is to ensure that uncertainty
never exceeds the institution’s ability
to understand,
govern,
and responsibly manage it.

9. AUTHORITY

This document defines the constitutional
Risk Governance model of COREI.

Every Risk Framework,

Architecture,

Engineering Practice,

Operational Capability,

Artificial Intelligence System,

Research Initiative,

Platform,

and future institutional capability

shall comply with this constitutional model.

Changes to this document require
constitutional review.
