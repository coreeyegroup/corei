COREI IMPLEMENTATION VALIDATION AND CERTIFICATION MODEL

COREI-IM-08.04

IMPLEMENTATION VALIDATION AND CERTIFICATION MODEL

1. PURPOSE

The COREI Implementation Validation and
Certification Model defines the authoritative
institutional method for proving that implemented
system capability is correct,
integrated,
architecture-faithful,
operationally usable,
and eligible for declared completion.

Its purpose is to ensure that implementation
completion is based upon evidence rather than

file existence,

command success,

build success,

developer confidence,

AI confidence,

visual appearance,

or conversational declaration.

Every material implementation state SHALL be
validated according to its actual risk,
scope,
and authority.

Certification SHALL represent a controlled
institutional declaration of proven state.

2. SCOPE

This document governs validation and certification
of

repositories,

directories,

files,

contracts,

platforms,

services,

modules,

engines,

domains,

applications,

workspaces,

user interfaces,

infrastructure,

configuration,

schemas,

data,

security,

identity,

trading systems,

risk systems,

portfolio systems,

execution systems,

automation,

artificial intelligence systems,

operational tooling,

migrations,

changes,

Stages,

Phases,

Steps,

and future institutional capability.

It governs

validation authority,

validation planning,

validation levels,

validation evidence,

failure classification,

revalidation,

certification,

conditional certification,

revocation,

and continuity.

3. VALIDATION PRINCIPLE

Every material implementation SHALL follow

Requirement

↓

Implementation

↓

Validation Criteria

↓

Validation Execution

↓

Evidence

↓

Result

↓

Certification Decision

Implementation and validation SHALL remain
distinct activities even when performed by the
same authorized actor.

4. VALIDATION IS PROOF

Validation SHALL answer

Did the implemented system achieve the required
state?

Validation SHALL be based upon observable evidence.

Assumption is not validation.

5. CERTIFICATION IS DECLARATION

Certification is the institutional declaration
that a defined implementation boundary has
satisfied its required validation criteria.

Certification SHALL identify

what is certified,

against what criteria,

using what evidence,

with what limitations,

and at what state.

6. VALIDATION AUTHORITY

Validation authority SHALL derive from

approved architecture,

approved specifications,

approved implementation requirements,

approved operational requirements,

approved security requirements,

approved trading controls,

and institutional governance.

A validator SHALL not invent acceptance criteria
after implementation merely to produce a passing
result.

7. VALIDATION OWNER

Every material validation activity SHALL have an
owner.

The validation owner is responsible for

criteria,

execution integrity,

evidence,

result classification,

and unresolved failures.

Validation execution MAY be delegated.

8. VALIDATION INDEPENDENCE

Validation SHOULD be sufficiently independent from
implementation to detect false assumptions.

Independence MAY be achieved through

separate commands,

separate tests,

separate environments,

separate reviewers,

automated controls,

or independent evidence.

Complete organizational separation is not always
required.

Evidence separation is required.

9. VALIDATION OBJECT

Every validation SHALL define the object being
validated.

The object MAY be

file,

contract,

package,

repository,

platform,

service,

module,

engine,

domain,

application,

workspace,

deployment,

migration,

workflow,

Stage,

Phase,

or Step.

Undefined validation boundaries are prohibited.

10. VALIDATION BASELINE

Validation SHALL identify the expected baseline.

The baseline MAY derive from

specification,

architecture,

contract,

previous certified state,

test fixture,

operational threshold,

or approved target state.

11. VALIDATION CRITERIA

Validation criteria SHALL be defined before
certification.

Criteria SHALL be

observable,

relevant,

repeatable where practical,

and sufficient to prove the required state.

Vague criteria such as

“looks good”

or

“should work”

are insufficient.

12. VALIDATION SCOPE

Validation scope SHALL correspond to implementation
scope and impact.

A local file change MAY require broader validation
if it affects

shared contracts,

platform consumers,

runtime behaviour,

data,

security,

or trading execution.

13. VALIDATION DEPTH

Validation depth SHALL be proportional to

risk,

criticality,

blast radius,

novelty,

and reversibility.

Critical financial or security capability SHALL
receive deeper validation than low-risk isolated
presentation changes.

14. VALIDATION LEVELS

COREI validation MAY operate at the following
levels.

Artifact Validation

Component Validation

Subsystem Validation

Integration Validation

Repository Validation

Architecture Validation

Runtime Validation

Operational Validation

Stage Validation

Institutional Certification

Not every implementation requires every level.

Required levels SHALL be determined by impact.

15. ARTIFACT VALIDATION

Artifact Validation proves the correctness of an
individual implementation artifact.

It MAY validate

syntax,

type correctness,

schema correctness,

configuration correctness,

or expected local behaviour.

16. COMPONENT VALIDATION

Component Validation proves that a coherent
component performs its defined responsibility.

It SHALL consider

inputs,

outputs,

state,

errors,

and dependencies

where applicable.

17. SUBSYSTEM VALIDATION

Subsystem Validation proves that multiple owned
components operate correctly as a coherent
subsystem.

It SHALL validate internal composition and public
contract behaviour.

18. INTEGRATION VALIDATION

Integration Validation proves that independently
implemented capabilities interact correctly.

It SHALL validate

registration,

discovery,

contracts,

data flow,

event flow,

command flow,

query flow,

state interaction,

and failure behaviour

where applicable.

19. REPOSITORY VALIDATION

Repository Validation proves that repository state
is structurally and technically valid.

It SHALL consider

directory structure,

file placement,

ownership,

public APIs,

imports,

exports,

dependencies,

cycles,

build,

and tests.

20. ARCHITECTURE VALIDATION

Architecture Validation proves that implementation
preserves approved

ownership,

boundaries,

dependency direction,

platform consumption,

domain separation,

application composition,

and runtime relationships.

A technically functioning system MAY fail
architecture validation.

21. RUNTIME VALIDATION

Runtime Validation proves that implemented
capability functions in an actual executable
environment.

Runtime validation MAY include

startup,

registration,

dependency resolution,

execution,

state transitions,

failure handling,

restart,

and shutdown.

22. OPERATIONAL VALIDATION

Operational Validation proves that the system can
be safely operated.

It MAY validate

health,

metrics,

logs,

traces,

alerts,

capacity,

recovery,

operator workflows,

and runbooks.

23. STAGE VALIDATION

Stage Validation proves that all required
implementation within a Stage has satisfied its
defined completion criteria.

A Stage SHALL not be certified merely because its
final Step was executed.

24. INSTITUTIONAL CERTIFICATION

Institutional Certification is the highest
applicable declaration that a defined capability
has satisfied all required implementation,
validation,
governance,
and operational criteria.

Certification SHALL remain bounded to the exact
certified state.

Material implementation MAY require validation

26. BUILD VALIDATION

Build Validation SHALL prove that required
implementation can be transformed into its expected
build artifact or executable state.

It MAY include

dependency installation,

compilation,

bundling,

generation,

and packaging.

Build success alone is insufficient for
certification.

27. TYPE VALIDATION

Typed systems SHALL pass required type validation.

Type errors SHALL not be hidden through

unsafe casts,

suppression,

or disabled checking

without explicit justification.

28. REPOSITORY STRUCTURE VALIDATION

Repository Structure Validation SHALL verify

correct directories,

correct file placement,

correct naming,

clear ownership,

and absence of prohibited structural sprawl.

29. PUBLIC API VALIDATION

Public API Validation SHALL verify

required exports,

contract accessibility,

absence of unintended exposure,

and consumer compatibility.

30. IMPORT VALIDATION

Import Validation SHALL verify that imports

resolve,

respect public boundaries,

and follow approved dependency direction.

31. EXPORT VALIDATION

Export Validation SHALL verify that

required capability is exported,

internal implementation remains protected,

and public surface is intentional.

32. DEPENDENCY VALIDATION

Dependency Validation SHALL verify

declared dependencies,

permitted direction,

version compatibility,

and absence of unauthorized coupling.

33. CIRCULAR DEPENDENCY VALIDATION

Circular Dependency Validation SHALL identify
dependency cycles.

Every detected material cycle SHALL be

removed,

explicitly justified,

or classified as transitional.

Unknown cycles SHALL fail certification.

34. ARCHITECTURE OWNERSHIP VALIDATION

Ownership Validation SHALL verify that capability
resides within the correct architectural owner.

Working code in the wrong owner SHALL not pass
architecture certification.

35. PLATFORM CONSUMPTION VALIDATION

Platform Consumption Validation SHALL verify that
consumers use approved platform capability rather
than recreate foundational infrastructure.

Duplicate platform foundation SHALL fail
certification unless explicitly authorized.

36. DOMAIN BOUNDARY VALIDATION

Domain Boundary Validation SHALL verify that

domain authority remains explicit,

cross-domain interaction uses approved contracts,

and one domain does not directly own another
domain’s internal state.

37. APPLICATION BOUNDARY VALIDATION

Application Boundary Validation SHALL verify that
applications compose approved capability without
absorbing unauthorized

platform,

domain,

or infrastructure

ownership.

38. CONTRACT VALIDATION

Contract Validation SHALL verify

contract definition,

provider implementation,

consumer expectations,

version compatibility,

and error behaviour.

39. SCHEMA VALIDATION

Schema Validation SHALL verify

syntax,

structure,

required fields,

types,

compatibility,

versioning,

and ownership

where applicable.

40. CONFIGURATION VALIDATION

Configuration Validation SHALL verify

required keys,

valid values,

environment applicability,

source authority,

and absence of prohibited secrets.

41. SECURITY VALIDATION

Security Validation SHALL verify applicable

authentication,

authorization,

least privilege,

secret handling,

trust boundaries,

input handling,

policy enforcement,

audit,

and failure behaviour.

42. DATA VALIDATION

Data Validation SHALL verify applicable

completeness,

correctness,

integrity,

consistency,

freshness,

ordering,

schema compliance,

and reconciliation.

43. INTEGRATION VALIDATION

Integration Validation SHALL prove that capability
is actually connected.

A file existing in the repository SHALL not prove
integration.

Integration SHALL verify required

registration,

routing,

dependency injection,

event subscription,

service discovery,

or composition.

44. BEHAVIOUR VALIDATION

Behaviour Validation SHALL verify expected
functional outcomes.

It SHALL test what the system does,
not merely whether implementation exists.

45. POSITIVE VALIDATION

Positive Validation SHALL verify expected behaviour
under valid conditions.

Successful-path testing is necessary but not always
sufficient.

46. NEGATIVE VALIDATION

Negative Validation SHALL verify behaviour under

invalid input,

missing dependency,

unauthorized action,

invalid state,

or prohibited operation

where applicable.

47. BOUNDARY VALIDATION

Boundary Validation SHALL verify behaviour at

minimum values,

maximum values,

thresholds,

empty states,

and transition points

where applicable.

48. FAILURE VALIDATION

Failure Validation SHALL prove that the system
fails safely and predictably.

It MAY validate

timeouts,

dependency failure,

partial failure,

invalid state,

restart,

retry,

and fallback.

49. PERFORMANCE VALIDATION

Performance Validation SHALL verify required

latency,

throughput,

resource consumption,

and scalability

where performance is material.

50. RESILIENCE VALIDATION

Resilience Validation SHALL verify behaviour under

dependency loss,

service restart,

network interruption,

resource pressure,

and partial degradation

where applicable.

51. RECOVERY VALIDATION

Recovery Validation SHALL prove that the system can
return to a controlled state after failure.

Recovery procedures SHALL be tested where risk
requires.

52. RESTART VALIDATION

Stateful or critical systems SHOULD validate
restart behaviour.

Restart SHALL not create

duplicate action,

state loss,

uncontrolled replay,

or inconsistent ownership.

53. IDEMPOTENCY VALIDATION

Operations required to be idempotent SHALL be
validated under repetition.

Repeated execution SHALL not create unintended
duplicate effects.

54. DETERMINISM VALIDATION

Deterministic capability SHALL produce predictable
results for equivalent authoritative inputs and
state.

Sources of uncontrolled nondeterminism SHALL be
identified.

55. OBSERVABILITY VALIDATION

Observability Validation SHALL verify that required

logs,

metrics,

traces,

events,

and health signals

are available and meaningful.

56. ALERT VALIDATION

Critical alerts SHOULD be validated to confirm that

the condition is detectable,

the alert is emitted,

and the alert is actionable.

57. OPERATOR VALIDATION

Operator-facing capability SHALL be validated
against actual operational workflows.

Technical correctness SHALL not compensate for an
unusable critical operator path.

58. VISUAL VALIDATION

Visual Validation SHALL verify

rendering,

layout,

hierarchy,

state representation,

interaction,

responsive behaviour,

and expected operator experience

where applicable.

59. TRADING VALIDATION

Trading Validation SHALL verify applicable
behaviour across

Market Intelligence

↓

Signal Intelligence

↓

Decision Intelligence

↓

Risk Intelligence

↓

Portfolio Intelligence

↓

Execution Intelligence

↓

Position Intelligence

↓

Performance Intelligence

↓

System Intelligence

No trading capability SHALL be certified solely
from isolated algorithm output.

60. MARKET DATA VALIDATION

Market data validation SHALL consider

source identity,

timestamps,

freshness,

completeness,

continuity,

ordering,

duplicates,

schema,

and quality.

61. SIGNAL VALIDATION

Signal validation SHALL verify

input integrity,

calculation correctness,

determinism,

timestamp alignment,

and output contract.

62. DECISION VALIDATION

Decision validation SHALL verify

decision inputs,

decision rules,

state,

constraints,

and resulting authority.

63. RISK VALIDATION

Risk validation SHALL verify that required limits
and controls cannot be bypassed by normal execution
paths.

Failure behaviour SHALL be explicit.

64. PORTFOLIO VALIDATION

Portfolio validation SHALL verify applicable

capital,

allocation,

exposure,

aggregation,

currency,

account,

strategy,

and portfolio

state.

65. EXECUTION VALIDATION

Execution validation SHALL verify applicable

order creation,

routing,

broker interaction,

acknowledgment,

rejection,

fill,

partial fill,

cancel,

retry,

and reconciliation.

66. POSITION VALIDATION

Position validation SHALL verify that internal
position state reconciles with authoritative
execution and broker state.

67. PERFORMANCE VALIDATION FOR TRADING

Trading performance validation SHALL distinguish

strategy performance

from

system correctness.

A profitable result SHALL not prove implementation
correctness.

An unprofitable result SHALL not by itself prove a
technical defect.

68. PAPER TRADING VALIDATION

Paper trading validation SHALL prove system
behaviour in a non-live execution environment.

It MAY validate

orders,

state transitions,

risk,

portfolio,

execution flow,

and reconciliation.

Paper validation SHALL not automatically certify
live trading.

69. LIVE TRADING VALIDATION

Live trading validation SHALL occur only under
explicit authority.

It SHALL use controlled

capital,

exposure,

instruments,

accounts,

and observation

appropriate to risk.

70. RECONCILIATION VALIDATION

Reconciliation Validation SHALL compare internal
state with authoritative external or upstream
state.

Differences SHALL be

explained,

corrected,

or explicitly accepted.

71. CONTINUITY VALIDATION

Continuity Validation SHALL verify that another
authorized engineer or AI system can determine

what was implemented,

where it exists,

how it is validated,

what remains unresolved,

and what happens next.

72. VALIDATION PLAN

A material implementation SHOULD define a
Validation Plan.

The plan MAY include

Validation Object

Requirements

Criteria

Commands

Tests

Environment

Evidence

Expected Result

Failure Conditions

Certification Gate

73. VALIDATION COMMAND

Validation commands SHOULD be exact and
reproducible.

A validation instruction SHALL identify

where the command runs,

what it validates,

and what success means.

74. VALIDATION ENVIRONMENT

Validation SHALL occur in an environment suitable
for the claim being made.

Development validation SHALL not automatically
prove

staging readiness,

production readiness,

or live trading readiness.

75. ENVIRONMENT PARITY

Where environment differences may affect behaviour,
validation SHALL account for those differences.

False parity SHALL not be assumed.

76. VALIDATION DATA

Validation data SHALL be appropriate to the
behaviour being tested.

Data SHOULD include applicable

normal cases,

edge cases,

invalid cases,

historical cases,

and failure cases.

77. TEST FIXTURE

Test fixtures SHALL be

controlled,

understandable,

and reproducible

where practical.

Fixtures SHALL not silently depend upon mutable
external state.

78. MOCK VALIDATION

Mocks MAY validate isolated behaviour.

Mock success SHALL not replace required integration
validation against real dependencies.

79. SIMULATION VALIDATION

Simulation MAY validate behaviour under controlled
conditions.

Simulation SHALL identify assumptions and
limitations.

80. REAL DEPENDENCY VALIDATION

Critical integrations SHOULD be validated against
real dependencies before production certification
where practical and safe.

81. VALIDATION EVIDENCE

Every material validation result SHALL be supported
by evidence.

Evidence MAY include

command output,

test results,

build output,

dependency reports,

logs,

metrics,

traces,

screenshots,

database queries,

broker responses,

reconciliation reports,

and signed records.

82. EVIDENCE QUALITY

Validation evidence SHALL be

relevant,

attributable,

understandable,

and sufficiently complete.

Evidence volume SHALL not substitute for evidence
quality.

83. EVIDENCE IDENTITY

Material evidence SHOULD identify

validation object,

time,

environment,

implementation state,

and result

where applicable.

84. EVIDENCE IMMUTABILITY

Critical certification evidence SHOULD be protected
from uncontrolled modification.

The required level of immutability SHALL reflect
risk.

85. EVIDENCE RETENTION

Evidence retention SHALL follow institutional
requirements.

Critical evidence SHOULD remain available for

audit,

incident investigation,

reconstruction,

and continuity.

86. VALIDATION RESULT

A validation result SHALL be classified.

Permitted result states MAY include

PASS

FAIL

BLOCKED

NOT_APPLICABLE

CONDITIONAL_PASS

UNKNOWN

The meaning SHALL be explicit.

87. PASS

PASS means all required criteria for the validation
object were satisfied.

PASS SHALL be supported by evidence.

88. FAIL

FAIL means one or more required criteria were not
satisfied.

A failed mandatory criterion SHALL block
certification unless explicitly governed by an
authorized exception.

89. BLOCKED

BLOCKED means validation cannot be completed due to
an unresolved dependency or condition.

Blocked validation SHALL not be reported as pass.

90. NOT APPLICABLE

NOT_APPLICABLE means a validation criterion does
not apply to the defined implementation object.

The reason SHOULD be explicit for material criteria.

91. CONDITIONAL PASS

CONDITIONAL_PASS means the required core criteria
are satisfied but explicit bounded conditions
remain.

Conditional pass SHALL identify

conditions,

risk,

owner,

and expiry or resolution criteria.

It SHALL not be used to hide mandatory failures.

92. UNKNOWN

UNKNOWN means the validation state cannot be
determined.

Unknown mandatory validation SHALL block
certification.

93. VALIDATION FAILURE CLASSIFICATION

Validation failures SHOULD be classified as

Implementation Failure

Integration Failure

Architecture Failure

Dependency Failure

Configuration Failure

Environment Failure

Test Failure

Evidence Failure

Requirement Ambiguity

External Dependency Failure

Classification SHALL guide correction.

94. IMPLEMENTATION FAILURE

Implementation Failure means the implemented
artifact does not satisfy required behaviour.

The implementation SHALL be corrected.

95. INTEGRATION FAILURE

Integration Failure means the capability exists but
is not correctly connected or consumed.

File existence SHALL not override the failure.

96. ARCHITECTURE FAILURE

Architecture Failure means the implementation
violates approved

ownership,

boundary,

dependency,

or composition.

Working behaviour SHALL not automatically override
architecture failure.

97. DEPENDENCY FAILURE

Dependency Failure means required dependency state
is unavailable,
incorrect,
or incompatible.

The dependency SHALL be resolved or the validation
classified as blocked.

98. CONFIGURATION FAILURE

Configuration Failure means the implementation
cannot satisfy required behaviour because
configuration is

missing,

invalid,

inconsistent,

or unauthorized.

99. ENVIRONMENT FAILURE

Environment Failure means the validation
environment cannot support a valid test.

The implementation SHALL not be falsely marked
failed when evidence proves the environment is the
cause.

The validation SHALL remain incomplete until
resolved.

100. TEST FAILURE

A test failure SHALL be investigated.

The cause MAY be

implementation defect,

test defect,

fixture defect,

environment defect,

or obsolete expectation.

Tests SHALL not be changed merely to obtain a pass.

101. EVIDENCE FAILURE

Evidence Failure means a claimed validation result
cannot be sufficiently proven.

Unproven success SHALL not be certified.

102. REQUIREMENT AMBIGUITY

If validation reveals ambiguous requirements,
implementation SHALL not invent the acceptance
criteria.

The ambiguity SHALL be resolved through appropriate
authority.

103. VALIDATION CORRECTION LOOP

Failed validation SHALL follow

Failure Detected

↓

Failure Classified

↓

Root Cause Identified

↓

Correct Owner Identified

↓

Correction Implemented

↓

Affected Validation Repeated

↓

Regression Validation Executed

↓

Result Updated

104. REVALIDATION

Revalidation SHALL occur when a material change may
invalidate previous evidence.

Revalidation scope SHALL reflect impact.

105. FULL REVALIDATION

Full revalidation MAY be required after

major architecture change,

critical dependency change,

large migration,

security incident,

or material environment change.

106. TARGETED REVALIDATION

Targeted revalidation MAY be used when impact is
well bounded.

The reason for limiting scope SHALL be defensible.

107. REGRESSION VALIDATION

Regression Validation SHALL verify that required
existing behaviour remains correct after change.

Regression scope SHALL include affected consumers
where necessary.

108. VALIDATION DRIFT

Validation Drift occurs when validation no longer
proves the current implementation state.

Drift MAY result from

code changes,

dependency changes,

environment changes,

configuration changes,

schema changes,

or obsolete tests.

Drift SHALL trigger revalidation.

109. STALE EVIDENCE

Evidence SHALL not be reused when the implementation
state has materially changed beyond its validity.

Certification SHALL not rely on stale evidence.

110. CERTIFICATION OBJECT

Every certification SHALL define exactly what is
being certified.

The object MAY be

artifact,

subsystem,

repository,

platform,

Phase,

Stage,

release,

deployment,

or operational state.

111. CERTIFICATION SCOPE

Certification SHALL apply only to the validated
scope.

Certification of one subsystem SHALL not imply
certification of the entire platform.

112. CERTIFICATION BASELINE

Certification SHOULD identify the exact
implementation baseline.

The baseline MAY include

repository commit,

release version,

deployment revision,

configuration version,

schema version,

or environment state.

113. CERTIFICATION CRITERIA

Certification criteria SHALL derive from required

implementation,

architecture,

engineering,

security,

operational,

and domain-specific

requirements.

114. CERTIFICATION STATUS

Certification status MAY include

CERTIFIED

CONDITIONALLY_CERTIFIED

NOT_CERTIFIED

CERTIFICATION_BLOCKED

CERTIFICATION_REVOKED

EXPIRED

Status SHALL be explicit.

115. CERTIFIED

CERTIFIED means all mandatory certification
criteria have been satisfied for the defined
scope and baseline.

116. CONDITIONALLY CERTIFIED

CONDITIONALLY_CERTIFIED means certification is
granted with explicit bounded conditions.

The conditions SHALL define

limitation,

risk,

owner,

resolution,

and expiration

where applicable.

117. NOT CERTIFIED

NOT_CERTIFIED means the object has not satisfied
required certification criteria.

Operational use SHALL follow the restrictions
appropriate to that state.

118. CERTIFICATION BLOCKED

CERTIFICATION_BLOCKED means certification cannot be
completed because a required dependency or
validation remains unresolved.

119. CERTIFICATION REVOKED

CERTIFICATION_REVOKED means a previously certified
state is no longer trusted.

Revocation MAY result from

critical defect,

security compromise,

invalid evidence,

material drift,

or failed revalidation.

120. CERTIFICATION EXPIRY

Certification MAY expire where periodic
revalidation is required.

Expiry SHALL be defined by policy or risk.

121. CERTIFICATION DECISION

Certification decisions SHALL be based on

required criteria,

validation results,

evidence,

known limitations,

and authority.

Certification SHALL not be granted to preserve
schedule appearance.

122. CERTIFICATION AUTHORITY

Certification authority SHALL correspond to the
scope being certified.

A subsystem owner MAY certify within granted
authority.

Institutional or production certification MAY
require higher authority.

123. SELF-CERTIFICATION

Self-certification MAY be permitted for bounded
low-risk implementation.

It SHALL still require objective evidence.

Self-certification SHALL not bypass required
independent controls.

124. ARCHITECTURE CERTIFICATION

Architecture Certification SHALL verify

Current Architecture

Target Architecture

Ownership

Dependency Direction

Public Boundaries

Integration

and absence of blocking architecture drift.

125. REPOSITORY CERTIFICATION

Repository Certification SHALL verify

Repository Audit

Directory Audit

Ownership Audit

Dependency Audit

Circular Dependency Audit

Public API Audit

Build Validation

Current State

and required architecture alignment.

126. IMPLEMENTATION CERTIFICATION

Implementation Certification SHALL verify that
required capability has been

created or updated,

integrated,

validated,

and documented

according to approved requirements.

127. OPERATIONAL CERTIFICATION

Operational Certification SHALL verify that the
system can be operated safely within its approved
environment.

It SHALL consider

observability,

failure handling,

recovery,

and operator readiness.

128. SECURITY CERTIFICATION

Security Certification SHALL verify applicable
security requirements for the defined scope.

Security certification SHALL not be inferred from
functional success.

129. TRADING CERTIFICATION

Trading Certification SHALL distinguish

technical correctness,

paper trading readiness,

controlled live readiness,

and unrestricted live authority.

These states SHALL not be conflated.

130. PHASE CERTIFICATION

A Phase SHALL be certified only when

all required Steps are complete,

required artifacts exist,

integration is complete,

validation passes,

known blockers are resolved,

and the Phase completion criteria are satisfied.

131. STAGE CERTIFICATION

A Stage SHALL be certified only when

all required Phases are complete,

cross-Phase integration is validated,

Stage-level criteria pass,

and the Stage produces its required institutional
capability.

135. CERTIFICATION CONTINUITY

Certification state SHALL survive

session changes,

team changes,

tool changes,

and AI system changes.

Certification SHALL not exist only in conversation.

136. CERTIFICATION SUPERSESSION

A new certification MAY supersede a previous
certification.

The relationship SHALL remain traceable.

137. CERTIFICATION REVOCATION

Certification SHALL be revoked when material
evidence proves that the certified claim is no
longer valid.

Revocation SHALL identify

reason,

affected scope,

operational impact,

and required next action.

138. CERTIFICATION RESTORATION

Revoked certification MAY be restored only after

root cause correction,

required revalidation,

and a new certification decision.

139. CONDITIONAL CERTIFICATION CONTROL

Conditional certification SHALL not become a
permanent substitute for full completion.

Conditions SHALL be tracked to

resolution,

expiry,

or revocation.

140. CERTIFICATION AND RELEASE

Release and certification are distinct.

A release MAY exist without full institutional
certification.

A certification MAY apply to a release,
deployment,
or narrower capability.

The relationship SHALL be explicit.

141. CERTIFICATION AND DEPLOYMENT

Deployment success SHALL not automatically grant
certification.

Certification SHALL require the defined validation
evidence.

142. CERTIFICATION AND PRODUCTION

Production presence SHALL not imply certified
state.

Uncertified production state SHALL be explicitly
known and governed.

143. CERTIFICATION AND LIVE TRADING

Technical certification SHALL not automatically
grant live trading authority.

Live trading authority SHALL remain a separate
institutional control.

144. AUTOMATED VALIDATION

Validation SHOULD be automated where

repeatability,

speed,

and reliability

benefit.

Automation SHALL not replace required judgment for
architecture,
security,
or operational claims.

145. CONTINUOUS VALIDATION

Critical systems MAY perform continuous validation
of

health,

configuration,

dependencies,

security,

data,

and operational invariants.

Continuous validation SHALL detect drift.

A validation pipeline MAY execute

↓

↓

↓

↓

Architecture Validation

↓

Security Validation

↓

↓

The exact sequence SHALL reflect system needs.

147. VALIDATION GATE

A Validation Gate blocks progression until required
criteria pass.

Gates MAY exist between

Steps,

Phases,

Stages,

environments,

deployments,

or trading authority levels.

148. HARD GATE

A Hard Gate SHALL not be bypassed without explicit
higher authority.

Mandatory security,
data,
or trading controls

SHOULD use hard gates where appropriate.

149. SOFT GATE

A Soft Gate MAY permit progression with explicit
known conditions.

Soft gates SHALL not be used for mandatory
constitutional requirements.

150. MANUAL VALIDATION

Manual validation MAY be required for

visual behaviour,

operator workflows,

complex architecture,

or exceptional conditions.

Manual validation SHALL still produce evidence.

151. HUMAN VALIDATION

Human judgment MAY validate aspects not reliably
captured by automation.

Human judgment SHALL not replace objective tests
where objective tests are available and required.

152. AI VALIDATION

Artificial intelligence systems MAY execute and
analyze validation.

AI SHALL

use actual evidence,

distinguish assumption from result,

report failures,

avoid invented output,

and preserve traceability.

153. AI SHALL NOT CERTIFY BY CONFIDENCE

AI confidence SHALL not constitute certification.

Certification requires evidence.

154. AI SHALL NOT INVENT VALIDATION OUTPUT

AI SHALL not fabricate

command output,

test results,

build results,

logs,

metrics,

screenshots,

or operational state.

Unknown results SHALL remain unknown.

155. AI SHALL NOT HIDE FAILED VALIDATION

Failed validation SHALL be reported explicitly.

AI SHALL not reinterpret a failure as success to
continue execution.

156. AI SHALL NOT REDUCE CRITERIA AFTER FAILURE

Validation criteria SHALL not be weakened merely
because implementation failed to satisfy them.

Requirement change requires authority.

157. AI SHALL DISTINGUISH IMPLEMENTED FROM VALIDATED

AI SHALL distinguish

CREATED

IMPLEMENTED

INTEGRATED

VALIDATED

CERTIFIED

These states SHALL not be used interchangeably.

158. NO CERTIFICATION BY FILE EXISTENCE

A file existing SHALL not prove

correctness,

integration,

or readiness.

159. NO CERTIFICATION BY TYPE CHECK ALONE

Type validation SHALL not prove runtime behaviour.

160. NO CERTIFICATION BY BUILD ALONE

Build success SHALL not prove

architecture correctness,

integration,

security,

data correctness,

or operational readiness.

161. NO CERTIFICATION BY TEST COUNT

A large number of tests SHALL not prove sufficient
coverage.

Validation quality matters more than test count.

162. NO CERTIFICATION BY VISUAL APPEARANCE

A visually correct interface SHALL not prove
correct

state,

data,

security,

or business behaviour.

163. NO CERTIFICATION BY PROFIT

Profitable trading output SHALL not prove technical
correctness.

Trading implementation SHALL be validated
independently of financial outcome.

164. NO CERTIFICATION WITH UNKNOWN BLOCKERS

Unknown mandatory blockers SHALL prevent
certification.

165. NO FALSE PASS

A validation result SHALL not be marked PASS when

the command was not executed,

the evidence was not observed,

the environment was unknown,

or required criteria were skipped.

166. NO SILENT SKIP

Skipped validation SHALL be explicitly classified.

Mandatory validation SHALL not disappear from the
record.

167. NO STALE CERTIFICATION

Certification SHALL not be treated as permanently
valid after material implementation drift.

168. NO CERTIFICATION BEYOND SCOPE

A certification claim SHALL not exceed its
validated boundary.

169. NO PRODUCTION CERTIFICATION BY DEVELOPMENT

Development environment validation SHALL not
automatically certify production behaviour.

170. NO LIVE CERTIFICATION BY PAPER TRADING

Paper trading success SHALL not automatically
certify live execution.
