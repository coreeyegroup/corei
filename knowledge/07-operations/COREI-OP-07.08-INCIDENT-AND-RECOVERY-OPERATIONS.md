# =============================================================================

#

# 1. PURPOSE

#

# =============================================================================

The COREI Incident and Recovery Operations document
defines the authoritative institutional model for

detecting,

declaring,

classifying,

containing,

stabilizing,

recovering,

validating,

reconciling,

learning from,

and permanently resolving

operational incidents.

Its purpose is to ensure that failures do not become

hidden,

unowned,

unbounded,

unrecoverable,

or institutionally forgotten.

Incident response SHALL preserve

safety,

authority,

evidence,

continuity,

financial integrity,

data integrity,

security,

and institutional trust.

# =============================================================================

#

# 2. SCOPE

#

# =============================================================================

This document governs incidents and recovery across

infrastructure,

networks,

storage,

databases,

messaging,

platforms,

services,

applications,

data,

security,

identity,

providers,

brokers,

trading,

portfolios,

risk,

execution,

positions,

performance,

automation,

artificial intelligence systems,

operator environments,

and future institutional capability.

It governs

detection,

triage,

declaration,

classification,

ownership,

escalation,

containment,

stabilization,

recovery,

reconciliation,

validation,

communication,

evidence,

post-incident review,

corrective action,

and institutional learning.

# =============================================================================

#

# 3. INCIDENT OPERATIONS PRINCIPLE

#

# =============================================================================

Incident Operations SHALL operate according to the
chain

Detect

↓

Acknowledge

↓

Triage

↓

Declare

↓

Classify

↓

Assign Authority

↓

Contain

↓

Stabilize

↓

Recover

↓

Reconcile

↓

Validate

↓

Resume

↓

Review

↓

Correct

↓

Learn

↓

Improve

Every material transition SHALL remain traceable.

# =============================================================================

#

# 4. INCIDENT DEFINITION

#

# =============================================================================

An incident is an unplanned event or condition that

causes,

degrades,

threatens,

or creates uncertainty about

the safe,
correct,
secure,
available,
or authorized operation

of COREI capability.

An incident MAY exist even when systems remain
technically available.

# =============================================================================

#

# 5. INCIDENT IS NOT LIMITED TO OUTAGE

#

# =============================================================================

An incident MAY include

service outage,

degraded performance,

incorrect data,

stale data,

security compromise,

unauthorized access,

configuration drift,

broker discrepancy,

unknown order state,

position mismatch,

financial discrepancy,

risk-control failure,

or incorrect system behaviour.

Availability alone SHALL NOT define incident state.

# =============================================================================

#

# 6. INCIDENT OWNERSHIP

#

# =============================================================================

Every active incident SHALL have explicit ownership.

Ownership SHALL identify

Incident Commander

and

appropriate technical or domain owners.

An unowned active incident is an operational
failure.

# =============================================================================

#

# 7. INCIDENT AUTHORITY

#

# =============================================================================

Incident authority SHALL define who may

declare an incident,

change severity,

contain systems,

suspend capability,

invoke emergency controls,

authorize recovery,

approve resumption,

and close the incident.

Technical access SHALL NOT automatically create
incident authority.

# =============================================================================

#

# 8. INCIDENT COMMAND

#

# =============================================================================

Material incidents SHALL establish clear incident
command.

The Incident Commander SHALL coordinate

scope,

priorities,

ownership,

communication,

decisions,

containment,

recovery,

and closure.

Incident command SHALL reduce ambiguity.

# =============================================================================

#

# 9. INCIDENT COMMANDER

#

# =============================================================================

The Incident Commander SHALL own coordination of
the incident.

The Incident Commander SHALL NOT be required to
personally perform every technical action.

Responsibilities include

maintaining incident state,

assigning work,

tracking decisions,

coordinating escalation,

protecting recovery discipline,

and determining when closure criteria are met.

# =============================================================================

#

# 10. DOMAIN OWNERS

#

# =============================================================================

Domain owners SHALL provide authoritative expertise
for affected capability.

Domain owners MAY include

Infrastructure Owner

Platform Owner

Service Owner

Data Owner

Security Owner

Trading Owner

Risk Authority

Portfolio Authority

Execution Owner

Broker Owner

or other responsible authority.

Domain ownership SHALL remain explicit.

# =============================================================================

#

# 11. INCIDENT DETECTION

#

# =============================================================================

Incidents MAY be detected through

monitoring,

alerting,

operators,

users,

reconciliation,

security systems,

providers,

brokers,

automated controls,

or external notification.

Detection source SHALL be recorded where
applicable.

# =============================================================================

#

# 12. INCIDENT ACKNOWLEDGEMENT

#

# =============================================================================

Critical incident signals SHALL be acknowledged.

Acknowledgement SHALL indicate that a responsible
party has accepted investigation responsibility.

Acknowledgement SHALL NOT imply resolution.

# =============================================================================

#

# 13. INCIDENT TRIAGE

#

# =============================================================================

Triage SHALL determine

what happened,

what is affected,

when it started,

whether it is ongoing,

current impact,

potential impact,

affected authority,

affected dependencies,

and immediate safety requirements.

Unknown information SHALL remain explicitly
unknown.

# =============================================================================

#

# 14. INCIDENT DECLARATION

#

# =============================================================================

An incident SHALL be formally declared when
operational coordination is required.

Declaration SHALL establish

incident identity,

time,

initial scope,

initial severity,

ownership,

and communication context.

Delayed declaration SHALL not be used to preserve
false operational health.

# =============================================================================

#

# 15. INCIDENT IDENTITY

#

# =============================================================================

Every material incident SHALL have unique identity.

Incident identity SHALL support correlation across

alerts,

logs,

changes,

actions,

communications,

evidence,

recovery,

and post-incident review.

# =============================================================================

#

# 16. INCIDENT CLASSIFICATION

#

# =============================================================================

Incidents SHALL be classified according to
institutional impact.

Classification SHALL consider

availability,

correctness,

security,

data integrity,

financial impact,

trading impact,

customer or operator impact,

blast radius,

and recovery complexity.

# =============================================================================

#

# 17. INCIDENT SEVERITY

#

# =============================================================================

COREI MAY use severity levels such as

SEV-1 — Critical

SEV-2 — Major

SEV-3 — Significant

SEV-4 — Minor

The exact severity SHALL be based upon impact,
not organizational convenience.

# =============================================================================

#

# 18. SEV-1 CRITICAL INCIDENT

#

# =============================================================================

A SEV-1 incident MAY include

systemic production failure,

active security compromise,

uncontrolled trading,

material financial uncertainty,

widespread data corruption,

critical authority compromise,

or failure threatening institutional continuity.

SEV-1 incidents SHALL receive immediate
institutional response.

# =============================================================================

#

# 19. SEV-2 MAJOR INCIDENT

#

# =============================================================================

A SEV-2 incident MAY include

major service degradation,

material broker outage,

significant trading restriction,

important data failure,

major platform failure,

or serious but contained security impact.

SEV-2 incidents require urgent coordinated
response.

# =============================================================================

#

# 20. SEV-3 SIGNIFICANT INCIDENT

#

# =============================================================================

A SEV-3 incident MAY include

limited production degradation,

contained service failure,

non-systemic reconciliation issue,

or operational defect requiring coordinated
response.

SEV-3 incidents SHALL remain owned and tracked.

# =============================================================================

#

# 21. SEV-4 MINOR INCIDENT

#

# =============================================================================

A SEV-4 incident MAY include

limited impact,

low-risk degradation,

or minor operational interruption.

Minor SHALL NOT mean undocumented.

# =============================================================================

#

# 22. SEVERITY CHANGE

#

# =============================================================================

Incident severity MAY change as evidence develops.

Severity changes SHALL be

explicit,

time-stamped,

and attributable

where required.

Initial underestimation SHALL not block later
escalation.

# =============================================================================

#

# 23. BLAST RADIUS

#

# =============================================================================

Every material incident SHALL assess blast radius.

Blast radius MAY include

services,

platforms,

clusters,

accounts,

brokers,

strategies,

portfolios,

markets,

regions,

users,

data,

or security boundaries.

Unknown blast radius SHALL be treated cautiously.

# =============================================================================

#

# 24. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

#

# =============================================================================

Impact assessment SHALL consider

what has failed,

what may fail,

what state may be incorrect,

what authority may be compromised,

and what irreversible consequence may occur.

Potential impact SHALL matter before confirmed
damage exists.

# =============================================================================

#

# 25. INCIDENT PRIORITY

#

# =============================================================================

Incident priority SHALL follow institutional risk.

Priority SHOULD protect in order

human safety where applicable,

security,

capital,

financial integrity,

data integrity,

institutional authority,

critical continuity,

and service availability.

Availability SHALL not automatically receive the
highest priority.

# =============================================================================

#

# 26. CONTAINMENT

#

# =============================================================================

Containment SHALL limit further impact.

Containment MAY include

isolating systems,

revoking access,

blocking traffic,

disabling services,

suspending strategies,

blocking new orders,

entering close-only mode,

disconnecting providers,

or activating fallback.

Containment SHALL be deliberate.

# =============================================================================

#

# 27. CONTAINMENT BEFORE ROOT CAUSE

#

# =============================================================================

Complete root cause understanding SHALL NOT be
required before necessary containment.

Where continued operation creates unacceptable
risk,
containment SHALL take priority.

Containment actions SHALL remain traceable.

# =============================================================================

#

# 28. STABILIZATION

#

# =============================================================================

Stabilization establishes a controlled operational
state.

A stabilized system MAY remain

degraded,

restricted,

partially unavailable,

or operating under temporary controls.

Stable SHALL NOT automatically mean recovered.

# =============================================================================

#

# 29. TEMPORARY CONTROLS

#

# =============================================================================

Temporary controls MAY be used during incidents.

Every temporary control SHALL define

reason,

scope,

owner,

risk,

monitoring,

and removal condition.

Temporary controls SHALL NOT silently become
permanent architecture.

# =============================================================================

#

# 30. RECOVERY OBJECTIVE

#

# =============================================================================

Recovery SHALL restore trusted capability.

Recovery SHALL NOT merely restore process
availability.

Trusted recovery requires confidence in

identity,

configuration,

data,

state,

dependencies,

security,

and domain correctness.

# =============================================================================

#

# 31. RECOVERY AUTHORITY

#

# =============================================================================

Recovery actions SHALL be performed by authorized
owners.

Recovery authority SHALL distinguish

technical restoration,

domain validation,

and institutional resumption approval.

Technical recovery SHALL not automatically
authorize business resumption.

# =============================================================================

#

# 32. RECOVERY PLAN

#

# =============================================================================

Material incidents SHALL maintain an explicit
recovery plan.

The plan SHOULD define

target state,

recovery sequence,

owners,

dependencies,

validation,

rollback,

and resumption criteria.

Unstructured trial-and-error SHALL be avoided.

# =============================================================================

#

# 33. DEPENDENCY-AWARE RECOVERY

#

# =============================================================================

Recovery SHALL follow actual dependency order.

A typical sequence MAY include

Infrastructure

↓

Security and Identity

↓

Data and Storage

↓

Messaging

↓

Platform Runtime

↓

Services

↓

Domain Capability

↓

External Connectivity

↓

Reconciliation

↓

Readiness Validation

↓

Controlled Resumption

The exact sequence SHALL follow system reality.

# =============================================================================

#

# 34. RECOVERY POINT OBJECTIVE

#

# =============================================================================

Critical stateful capability SHOULD define a
Recovery Point Objective where appropriate.

RPO SHALL describe the maximum acceptable amount of
data loss measured in time or equivalent state.

RPO SHALL reflect institutional impact.

# =============================================================================

#

# 35. RECOVERY TIME OBJECTIVE

#

# =============================================================================

Critical capability SHOULD define a Recovery Time
Objective where appropriate.

RTO SHALL describe the target time to restore
required capability.

RTO SHALL not justify unsafe recovery.

# =============================================================================

#

# 36. RPO AND RTO REALISM

#

# =============================================================================

RPO and RTO SHALL reflect

architecture,

dependencies,

backup capability,

replication,

operational staffing,

and actual recovery procedures.

Unvalidated recovery objectives are assumptions.

# =============================================================================

#

# 37. RECOVERY FROM BACKUP

#

# =============================================================================

Backup restoration SHALL verify

backup identity,

backup integrity,

restore target,

data consistency,

security,

and application compatibility.

Successful restore command SHALL NOT alone prove
successful recovery.

# =============================================================================

#

# 38. STATE RECOVERY

#

# =============================================================================

State recovery SHALL identify authoritative state.

Authoritative state MAY exist in

database,

event log,

broker,

provider,

custodian,

or another external authority.

Recovery SHALL not create conflicting hidden state.

# =============================================================================

#

# 39. DATA RECOVERY

#

# =============================================================================

Data recovery SHALL preserve

integrity,

lineage,

time,

schema,

and authority.

Recovered data SHALL be validated before normal
consumption.

# =============================================================================

#

# 40. EVENT RECOVERY

#

# =============================================================================

Event-driven systems SHALL recover with explicit
understanding of

offsets,

ordering,

duplication,

replay,

idempotency,

and downstream state.

Replay SHALL not create unintended duplicate
effects.

# =============================================================================

#

# 41. SERVICE RECOVERY

#

# =============================================================================

Service recovery SHALL validate

process health,

dependencies,

configuration,

state,

security,

and domain behaviour.

A running service SHALL not automatically be
declared recovered.

# =============================================================================

#

# 42. PLATFORM RECOVERY

#

# =============================================================================

Platform recovery SHALL restore shared capability
without creating false downstream readiness.

Consumers SHALL validate their own domain readiness
after platform restoration.

# =============================================================================

#

# 43. INFRASTRUCTURE RECOVERY

#

# =============================================================================

Infrastructure recovery SHALL restore

compute,

network,

storage,

orchestration,

security foundations,

and observability

according to dependency order.

Infrastructure availability SHALL not automatically
mean application recovery.

# =============================================================================

#

# 44. SECURITY RECOVERY

#

# =============================================================================

Security recovery SHALL validate

identity,

access,

credentials,

secrets,

keys,

certificates,

controls,

and evidence.

Known compromise SHALL be removed before
unrestricted resumption.

# =============================================================================

#

# 45. TRADING RECOVERY

#

# =============================================================================

Trading recovery SHALL validate

market data,

broker connectivity,

orders,

executions,

positions,

balances,

margin,

risk,

portfolio state,

and trading readiness.

Trading SHALL NOT resume solely because services
are online.

# =============================================================================

#

# 46. ORDER RECOVERY

#

# =============================================================================

After execution-related incidents,
all material orders SHALL be reconciled.

Recovery SHALL identify

submitted orders,

acknowledged orders,

open orders,

partially filled orders,

filled orders,

cancelled orders,

rejected orders,

and unknown orders.

Unknown order state SHALL remain blocking where
financial risk exists.

# =============================================================================

#

# 47. POSITION RECOVERY

#

# =============================================================================

Position recovery SHALL establish authoritative
position state.

Recovery SHALL reconcile

internal positions,

broker positions,

and custodian positions

where applicable.

Unknown material position differences SHALL block
normal trading.

# =============================================================================

#

# 48. FINANCIAL RECOVERY

#

# =============================================================================

Financial recovery SHALL validate

cash,

balances,

margin,

collateral,

fees,

PnL,

and other consequential financial state

where applicable.

Technical recovery SHALL not conceal financial
discrepancy.

# =============================================================================

#

# 49. RECONCILIATION

#

# =============================================================================

Reconciliation is a mandatory recovery control.

Reconciliation SHALL compare internal state with
authoritative external or independent state where
applicable.

Unknown discrepancy SHALL remain visible.

# =============================================================================

#

# 50. RECOVERY VALIDATION

#

# =============================================================================

Recovery SHALL be validated.

Validation SHALL determine whether

the original failure is contained,

required capability is restored,

state is correct,

security is intact,

dependencies are healthy,

and residual risk is acceptable.

Validation SHALL be evidence-based.

# =============================================================================

#

# 51. RESUMPTION

#

# =============================================================================

Resumption is the controlled return to normal
operation.

Resumption MAY be

partial,

restricted,

staged,

or complete.

Resumption SHALL require appropriate authority.

# =============================================================================

#

# 52. STAGED RESUMPTION

#

# =============================================================================

High-risk capability SHOULD resume progressively
where appropriate.

Stages MAY include

Observe Only

Read Only

Restricted Operation

Close-Only

Limited Production

Normal Production

Progression SHALL follow validation evidence.

# =============================================================================

#

# 53. RESUMPTION AUTHORITY

#

# =============================================================================

The authority to resume SHALL reflect the affected
domain.

Examples include

Security Authority

Infrastructure Authority

Service Owner

Data Authority

Risk Authority

Trading Authority

or Executive Authority

where required.

Technical restoration SHALL not bypass domain
authority.

# =============================================================================

#

# 54. INCIDENT COMMUNICATION

#

# =============================================================================

Incident communication SHALL be

accurate,

timely,

controlled,

role-appropriate,

and evidence-aware.

Communication SHALL distinguish

confirmed facts,

working hypotheses,

unknowns,

decisions,

and next actions.

# =============================================================================

#

# 55. INCIDENT STATUS

#

# =============================================================================

Material incidents SHALL maintain explicit status.

Status MAY include

Detected

Triaging

Declared

Contained

Stabilized

Recovering

Validating

Monitoring

Resolved

Closed

Status SHALL reflect actual state.

# =============================================================================

#

# 56. INCIDENT TIMELINE

#

# =============================================================================

Material incidents SHALL maintain a timeline.

The timeline SHOULD record

detection,

acknowledgement,

declaration,

severity changes,

major findings,

containment,

recovery actions,

validation,

resumption,

and closure.

The timeline SHALL support reconstruction.

# =============================================================================

#

# 57. DECISION LOG

#

# =============================================================================

Material incident decisions SHOULD be recorded.

Decision records SHOULD identify

time,

decision,

authority,

reason,

known evidence,

and expected effect.

Decision logging SHALL not obstruct urgent action.

# =============================================================================

#

# 58. ACTION LOG

#

# =============================================================================

Material incident actions SHALL be traceable.

Actions MAY include

commands,

configuration changes,

deployments,

access changes,

service restarts,

traffic changes,

trading controls,

and recovery actions.

Untracked emergency action creates recovery risk.

# =============================================================================

#

# 59. EVIDENCE PRESERVATION

#

# =============================================================================

Incident response SHALL preserve relevant evidence.

Evidence MAY include

logs,

metrics,

traces,

events,

configuration,

deployment history,

audit records,

database state,

broker records,

provider records,

and communications.

Evidence SHALL not be destroyed for convenience.

# =============================================================================

#

# 60. FORENSIC INTEGRITY

#

# =============================================================================

Where forensic investigation is required,
evidence SHALL preserve

identity,

time,

source,

integrity,

and handling history.

Recovery SHALL avoid unnecessary destruction of
forensic evidence.

# =============================================================================

#

# 61. CHANGE FREEZE

#

# =============================================================================

Material incidents MAY require change freeze.

A change freeze SHALL define

scope,

exceptions,

authority,

and removal condition.

Unrelated change SHALL not increase incident
uncertainty.

# =============================================================================

#

# 62. EMERGENCY CHANGE

#

# =============================================================================

Emergency change MAY be necessary during incident
response.

Emergency change SHALL still preserve

owner,

purpose,

scope,

risk,

action,

validation,

and follow-up.

Emergency SHALL NOT mean undocumented.

# =============================================================================

#

# 63. ROLLBACK

#

# =============================================================================

Rollback MAY be used where

the previous state is known,

rollback is safe,

state compatibility is understood,

and rollback reduces risk.

Rollback SHALL not be assumed safe merely because a
previous version existed.

# =============================================================================

#

# 64. ROLL FORWARD

#

# =============================================================================

Roll-forward MAY be preferable when

state has changed,

rollback is unsafe,

or the corrective version is lower risk.

Recovery strategy SHALL follow system reality.

# =============================================================================

#

# 65. FAILOVER

#

# =============================================================================

Failover SHALL be controlled.

Failover SHALL validate

target readiness,

data consistency,

identity,

networking,

dependencies,

and authority.

Automatic failover SHALL not create split-brain or
duplicate financial action.

# =============================================================================

#

# 66. FAILBACK

#

# =============================================================================

Failback SHALL be treated as a controlled change.

Failback SHALL validate

source readiness,

state synchronization,

traffic transition,

and recovery.

Restoring the original environment SHALL not be
assumed trivial.

# =============================================================================

#

# 67. DEGRADED MODE

#

# =============================================================================

A system MAY operate in degraded mode when

the mode is defined,

risk is understood,

authority approves,

and monitoring is sufficient.

Degraded mode SHALL be visible.

# =============================================================================

#

# 68. RESTRICTED MODE

#

# =============================================================================

Restricted mode SHALL allow only explicitly
permitted operations.

Restrictions MAY apply to

users,

services,

accounts,

brokers,

strategies,

markets,

or actions.

Restricted mode SHALL remain enforceable.

# =============================================================================

#

# 69. SAFE MODE

#

# =============================================================================

Critical systems MAY define a safe mode.

Safe mode SHALL prioritize preservation of

security,

capital,

data,

state,

and recoverability

over normal throughput or feature availability.

# =============================================================================

#

# 70. INCIDENT ESCALATION

#

# =============================================================================

Escalation SHALL be based upon

severity,

impact,

uncertainty,

duration,

authority,

financial risk,

security risk,

and recovery complexity.

Escalation SHALL not depend solely upon elapsed
time.

# =============================================================================

#

# 71. TECHNICAL ESCALATION

#

# =============================================================================

Technical escalation SHALL involve owners with
required system expertise.

Escalation SHALL preserve current findings and
actions.

New responders SHALL not need to reconstruct the
incident from memory.

# =============================================================================

#

# 72. DOMAIN ESCALATION

#

# =============================================================================

Domain escalation SHALL involve appropriate

data,

security,

risk,

portfolio,

trading,

or financial authority

when the incident crosses technical boundaries.

# =============================================================================

#

# 73. EXECUTIVE ESCALATION

#

# =============================================================================

Executive escalation MAY be required for

systemic incidents,

material financial impact,

critical security compromise,

institutional continuity risk,

or major external obligations.

Executive escalation SHALL receive concise,
decision-relevant information.

# =============================================================================

#

# 74. EXTERNAL ESCALATION

#

# =============================================================================

External escalation MAY involve

providers,

brokers,

custodians,

exchanges,

vendors,

or other authorized parties.

External dependency SHALL not remove internal
incident ownership.

# =============================================================================

#

# 75. INCIDENT HANDOVER

#

# =============================================================================

Incident responsibility transfer SHALL preserve

current status,

severity,

impact,

timeline,

hypotheses,

confirmed facts,

actions completed,

actions pending,

temporary controls,

risks,

and next decisions.

Handover SHALL be explicit.

# =============================================================================

#

# 76. 24X7 INCIDENT OPERATIONS

#

# =============================================================================

Where COREI operates continuously,
incident response SHALL support continuous
ownership.

No critical incident SHALL become unowned because
of time zone,
shift,
or operator availability.

# =============================================================================

#

# 77. INCIDENT FATIGUE

#

# =============================================================================

Long-running incidents SHALL manage responder
fatigue.

Incident command SHOULD rotate responsibilities
where necessary while preserving continuity.

Fatigue SHALL not silently degrade decision quality.

# =============================================================================

#

# 78. INCIDENT TOOLING

#

# =============================================================================

Incident tooling MAY support

alerts,

incident identity,

timeline,

communication,

runbooks,

evidence,

tasks,

and post-incident review.

Tool failure SHALL not eliminate the ability to
manage incidents.

# =============================================================================

#

# 79. INCIDENT AUTOMATION

#

# =============================================================================

Automation MAY support

detection,

enrichment,

routing,

containment,

evidence collection,

recovery,

and validation.

Automation SHALL remain bounded by explicit
authority.

# =============================================================================

#

# 80. AUTOMATED CONTAINMENT

#

# =============================================================================

Automated containment MAY be used where

trigger,

scope,

authority,

failure behaviour,

and reversal

are defined.

High-impact automated containment SHALL be tested.

# =============================================================================

#

# 81. AUTOMATED RECOVERY

#

# =============================================================================

Automated recovery MAY be used for well-understood
failure modes.

Automation SHALL validate successful recovery.

Repeated automatic restart without root cause
visibility SHALL not be considered resilience.

# =============================================================================

#

# 82. SELF-HEALING

#

# =============================================================================

Self-healing capability SHALL be

bounded,

observable,

deterministic where required,

and safe.

Self-healing SHALL not hide persistent structural
failure.

# =============================================================================

#

# 83. RECOVERY LOOP PROTECTION

#

# =============================================================================

Automated recovery SHALL prevent uncontrolled loops.

Repeated failed recovery attempts SHALL

stop,

escalate,

or enter safe state

according to policy.

Infinite recovery loops are prohibited.

# =============================================================================

#

# 84. RUNBOOKS

#

# =============================================================================

Critical incident classes SHALL maintain usable
runbooks.

Runbooks SHOULD define

trigger,

triage,

containment,

recovery,

validation,

escalation,

communication,

and closure.

Runbooks SHALL reflect actual architecture.

# =============================================================================

#

# 85. RUNBOOK EXECUTION

#

# =============================================================================

Runbook execution SHALL remain adaptable to
evidence.

Runbooks guide response.

They SHALL NOT require responders to ignore system
reality.

# =============================================================================

#

# 86. RUNBOOK VALIDATION

#

# =============================================================================

Critical runbooks SHALL be periodically validated.

Validation MAY include

tabletop exercises,

simulation,

staging tests,

or controlled production tests.

Untested recovery procedures SHALL be treated as
risk.

# =============================================================================

#

# 87. DISASTER RECOVERY

#

# =============================================================================

Disaster Recovery addresses failure exceeding
normal local recovery.

Disaster scenarios MAY include

site failure,

cluster loss,

storage loss,

regional outage,

major security compromise,

or systemic infrastructure failure.

Disaster recovery SHALL preserve institutional
authority.

# =============================================================================

#

# 88. BUSINESS CONTINUITY

#

# =============================================================================

Business continuity SHALL define how critical
institutional functions continue during disruption.

Continuity MAY use

alternate systems,

alternate providers,

alternate brokers,

manual controls,

restricted operation,

or temporary suspension.

Continuity SHALL not bypass mandatory safety
controls.

# =============================================================================

#

# 89. MINIMUM VIABLE OPERATING STATE

#

# =============================================================================

Critical domains SHOULD define the minimum viable
operating state required during major disruption.

The state SHALL identify

essential capability,

essential authority,

essential data,

essential controls,

and prohibited activity.

# =============================================================================

#

# 90. DISASTER DECLARATION

#

# =============================================================================

Disaster declaration SHALL be explicit.

Declaration SHALL identify

scope,

authority,

affected capability,

recovery strategy,

and communication model.

Disaster mode SHALL not be entered casually.

# =============================================================================

#

# 91. DISASTER RECOVERY SITE

#

# =============================================================================

Where alternate recovery environments exist,
they SHALL maintain appropriate

security,

configuration,

data,

capacity,

connectivity,

and validation.

An untested recovery site is not proven recovery
capability.

# =============================================================================

#

# 92. BACKUP OPERATIONS

#

# =============================================================================

Critical state SHALL have backup appropriate to its
recovery requirements.

Backup operations SHALL define

scope,

frequency,

retention,

security,

integrity,

and restore testing.

Backup success SHALL be observable.

# =============================================================================

#

# 93. RESTORE TESTING

#

# =============================================================================

Backups SHALL be periodically restored and
validated according to criticality.

A backup that has never been restored is not proven
recovery capability.

# =============================================================================

#

# 94. RECOVERY EXERCISES

#

# =============================================================================

COREI SHOULD conduct recovery exercises for
critical capability.

Exercises MAY validate

people,

authority,

runbooks,

tools,

dependencies,

communication,

and technical recovery.

Exercise findings SHALL produce improvement.

# =============================================================================

#

# 95. GAME DAYS

#

# =============================================================================

Controlled failure exercises MAY be used to test
resilience.

Game days SHALL be

authorized,

bounded,

observable,

and recoverable.

Testing SHALL not create uncontrolled production
risk.

# =============================================================================

#

# 96. CHAOS ENGINEERING

#

# =============================================================================

Chaos engineering MAY be used for mature capability
where appropriate.

Chaos experiments SHALL define

hypothesis,

scope,

blast radius,

safety controls,

observation,

and stop condition.

Random uncontrolled failure injection is
prohibited.

# =============================================================================

#

# 97. POST-INCIDENT REVIEW

#

# =============================================================================

Material incidents SHALL receive post-incident
review.

The review SHALL determine

what happened,

why it happened,

why controls did or did not work,

how response performed,

and what must change.

The objective is institutional improvement.

# =============================================================================

#

# 98. BLAMELESS DOES NOT MEAN ACCOUNTABILITY-FREE

#

# =============================================================================

Post-incident review SHOULD avoid unproductive
personal blame.

However,
blameless review SHALL NOT eliminate

ownership,

authority,

responsibility,

or corrective action.

System learning and accountability SHALL coexist.

# =============================================================================

#

# 99. ROOT CAUSE

#

# =============================================================================

Root cause analysis SHALL seek the conditions that
made the incident possible.

Root cause SHALL not stop at

human error,

service crashed,

or configuration changed.

Analysis SHALL examine

system design,

controls,

process,

authority,

observability,

and recovery.

# =============================================================================

#

# 100. CONTRIBUTING FACTORS

#

# =============================================================================

Incidents MAY have multiple contributing factors.

Factors MAY include

architecture,

implementation,

configuration,

dependency,

operational process,

monitoring,

documentation,

training,

or external systems.

Single-cause simplification SHALL be avoided.

# =============================================================================

#

# 101. FIVE WHYS

#

# =============================================================================

Techniques such as Five Whys MAY support analysis.

The technique SHALL not be used mechanically.

The objective is actionable systemic understanding.

# =============================================================================

#

# 102. CORRECTIVE ACTION

#

# =============================================================================

Post-incident corrective actions SHALL be

specific,

owned,

prioritized,

and tracked.

Actions MAY include

architecture correction,

implementation correction,

monitoring,

automation,

documentation,

testing,

or governance change.

# =============================================================================

#

# 103. CORRECTIVE ACTION PRIORITY

#

# =============================================================================

Corrective actions SHALL be prioritized according
to

recurrence risk,

impact,

exploitability,

financial risk,

security risk,

and systemic importance.

Cosmetic closure SHALL not replace meaningful
correction.

# =============================================================================

#

# 104. INCIDENT RECURRENCE

#

# =============================================================================

Repeated incidents of the same class SHALL trigger
deeper review.

Recurrence MAY indicate

structural defect,

insufficient corrective action,

weak ownership,

or ineffective controls.

Repeated manual recovery is not institutional
resilience.

# =============================================================================

#

# 105. KNOWN ERROR

#

# =============================================================================

A known unresolved defect causing recurring
incidents SHALL be explicitly tracked.

Known errors SHALL define

impact,

workaround,

risk,

owner,

and permanent correction plan.

Workaround SHALL not become invisible architecture.

# =============================================================================

#

# 106. INCIDENT DEBT

#

# =============================================================================

Unresolved incident findings SHALL create visible
operational debt.

Debt SHALL be

owned,

prioritized,

tracked,

and reviewed.

Incident closure SHALL not erase unresolved risk.

# =============================================================================

#

# 107. INCIDENT KNOWLEDGE

#

# =============================================================================

Incident knowledge SHALL be preserved
institutionally.

Knowledge SHOULD include

symptoms,

detection,

root cause,

containment,

recovery,

validation,

and prevention.

Critical recovery knowledge SHALL not depend upon
one individual.

# =============================================================================

#

# 108. INCIDENT METRICS

#

# =============================================================================

Incident metrics MAY include

incident count,

severity distribution,

time to detect,

time to acknowledge,

time to contain,

time to recover,

time to reconcile,

time to close,

recurrence rate,

and corrective action completion.

Metrics SHALL support improvement.

# =============================================================================

#

# 109. MEAN TIME TO DETECT

#

# =============================================================================

Mean Time to Detect MAY measure how quickly
incidents become visible.

Detection speed SHALL be interpreted with detection
quality.

# =============================================================================

#

# 110. MEAN TIME TO ACKNOWLEDGE

#

# =============================================================================

Mean Time to Acknowledge MAY measure how quickly
responsibility is accepted.

Fast acknowledgement without action SHALL not be
treated as successful response.

# =============================================================================

#

# 111. MEAN TIME TO CONTAIN

#

# =============================================================================

Mean Time to Contain MAY measure how quickly further
impact is limited.

Containment quality SHALL matter more than speed
alone.

# =============================================================================

#

# 112. MEAN TIME TO RECOVER

#

# =============================================================================

Mean Time to Recover MAY measure restoration of
trusted capability.

Service restart time SHALL not automatically equal
recovery time.

# =============================================================================

#

# 113. RECOVERY CONFIDENCE

#

# =============================================================================

Recovery confidence SHALL be based upon

validation,

reconciliation,

monitoring,

and evidence.

Confidence SHALL not be based solely upon absence
of new alerts.

# =============================================================================

#

# 114. INCIDENT AUDIT

#

# =============================================================================

Material incident activity SHALL preserve audit
evidence.

Audit MAY include

declaration,

severity changes,

authority changes,

containment,

emergency access,

configuration changes,

recovery,

resumption,

and closure.

# =============================================================================

#

# 115. INCIDENT CLOSURE

#

# =============================================================================

An incident SHALL be closed only when

active impact is resolved or accepted,

required recovery is complete,

state is reconciled,

residual risk is understood,

ownership of follow-up exists,

and evidence is preserved.

Closure SHALL not mean all corrective work is
complete.

# =============================================================================

#

# 116. RESOLVED VERSUS CLOSED

#

# =============================================================================

Resolved means active incident impact has ended.

Closed means incident handling requirements have
been completed.

These states SHALL remain distinct where useful.

# =============================================================================

#

# 117. INCIDENT REOPENING

#

# =============================================================================

An incident MAY be reopened when

impact returns,

recovery proves incomplete,

or new evidence materially changes understanding.

Reopening SHALL preserve original incident history.

# =============================================================================

#

# 118. EXTERNAL INCIDENTS

#

# =============================================================================

Provider,
broker,
exchange,
custodian,
or vendor incidents

SHALL still receive internal ownership.

External responsibility SHALL not eliminate
internal operational response.

# =============================================================================

#

# 119. PROVIDER INCIDENTS

#

# =============================================================================

Provider incidents SHALL assess

affected data,

affected services,

fallback,

staleness,

integrity,

and recovery.

Provider status communication SHALL not replace
internal validation.

# =============================================================================

#

# 120. BROKER INCIDENTS

#

# =============================================================================

Broker incidents SHALL assess

accounts,

orders,

executions,

positions,

balances,

margin,

and financial exposure.

Broker recovery SHALL be followed by reconciliation.

# =============================================================================

#

# 121. SECURITY INCIDENTS

#

# =============================================================================

Security incidents SHALL preserve

containment,

evidence,

credential control,

forensics,

eradication,

and trusted recovery.

Security recovery SHALL not sacrifice evidence
without explicit authority.

# =============================================================================

#

# 122. DATA INCIDENTS

#

# =============================================================================

Data incidents SHALL assess

authority,

quality,

freshness,

lineage,

integrity,

consumers,

and derived state.

Corrected source data MAY require downstream
recomputation.

# =============================================================================

#

# 123. TRADING INCIDENTS

#

# =============================================================================

Trading incidents SHALL prioritize

capital protection,

risk containment,

order certainty,

position certainty,

financial reconciliation,

and controlled resumption.

Unknown financial state SHALL remain explicit.

# =============================================================================

#

# 124. AI INCIDENTS

#

# =============================================================================

Artificial intelligence incidents MAY include

unsafe tool use,

incorrect authority,

untrusted context influence,

model failure,

provider failure,

or uncontrolled automation.

AI incident response SHALL preserve deterministic
institutional controls.

# =============================================================================

#

# 125. NO INCIDENT PATCH WORK

#

# =============================================================================

Incident Operations SHALL NOT permanently
compensate for structural defects through

operator memory,

repeated restarts,

manual reconciliation,

hidden scripts,

or undocumented emergency procedures.

Permanent correction SHALL occur at the proper
ownership layer.

# =============================================================================

#

# 126. NO FALSE RECOVERY

#

# =============================================================================

A system SHALL NOT be declared recovered solely
because

processes restarted,

health checks passed,

alerts stopped,

or dashboards appear normal.

Recovery SHALL require domain-valid state.

# =============================================================================

#

# 127. NO BLIND RESTART

#

# =============================================================================

Repeated restart without understanding state and
impact SHALL not be treated as recovery.

Restart MAY be appropriate.

Blind restart loops are prohibited.

# =============================================================================

#

# 128. NO HIDDEN UNKNOWN STATE

#

# =============================================================================

Unknown state SHALL remain explicit.

Unknown

order state,

position state,

data state,

security state,

or financial state

SHALL not be silently converted into assumed
normality.

# =============================================================================

#

# 129. NO UNOWNED INCIDENT

#

# =============================================================================

Every active material incident SHALL have an owner.

If ownership changes,
handover SHALL be explicit.

# =============================================================================

#

# 130. NO UNTRACKED EMERGENCY CHANGE

#

# =============================================================================

Emergency change SHALL remain traceable.

Urgency SHALL not remove institutional memory.

# =============================================================================

#

# 131. NO AVAILABILITY-ONLY RECOVERY

#

# =============================================================================

Availability SHALL NOT be the sole recovery
criterion.

Correctness,
security,
state,
authority,
and reconciliation

SHALL also be considered.

# =============================================================================

#

# 132. NO AUTOMATIC RESUMPTION OF HIGH-RISK ACTIVITY

#

# =============================================================================

High-risk activity SHALL NOT automatically resume
solely because a dependency recovered.

Trading,
privileged operations,
or other consequential capability

SHALL require readiness validation where mandated.

# =============================================================================

#

# 133. NO INCIDENT MEMORY LOSS

#

# =============================================================================

Incident knowledge SHALL not disappear after
closure.

Material incidents SHALL contribute to

documentation,

runbooks,

monitoring,

testing,

architecture,

and institutional knowledge.

# =============================================================================

#

# 134. INCIDENT LIFECYCLE

#

# =============================================================================

Every material incident follows the lifecycle

Detection

↓

Acknowledgement

↓

Triage

↓

Declaration

↓

Classification

↓

Ownership

↓

Containment

↓

Stabilization

↓

Recovery

↓

Reconciliation

↓

Validation

↓

Resumption

↓

Resolution

↓

Review

↓

Corrective Action

↓

Closure

↓

Institutional Learning

The lifecycle SHALL remain traceable.

# =============================================================================

#

# 135. RECOVERY LIFECYCLE

#

# =============================================================================

Every material recovery follows the lifecycle

Identify Trusted Target State

↓

Identify Authoritative State

↓

Establish Recovery Sequence

↓

Restore Dependencies

↓

Restore Capability

↓

Reconcile State

↓

Validate Security

↓

Validate Domain Correctness

↓

Validate Readiness

↓

Authorize Resumption

↓

Monitor

The lifecycle SHALL remain explicit.

# =============================================================================

#

# 136. INCIDENT EVOLUTION

#

# =============================================================================

Incident Operations SHALL evolve through

incident evidence,

recovery exercises,

architecture changes,

new dependencies,

security threats,

trading requirements,

and institutional growth.

Evolution SHALL preserve authority and traceability.

# =============================================================================

#

# 137. RELATIONSHIP TO PRODUCTION OPERATIONS

#

# =============================================================================

Production Operations defines the common
institutional operating model.

Incident and Recovery Operations defines how COREI
responds when normal operation fails or becomes
uncertain.

Every production capability SHALL comply with
applicable incident and recovery requirements.

# =============================================================================

#

# 138. RELATIONSHIP TO PLATFORM OPERATIONS

#

# =============================================================================

Platform incidents MAY affect many consumers.

Platform recovery SHALL restore shared capability.

Consumer domains SHALL independently validate their
own readiness before consequential resumption.

# =============================================================================

#

# 139. RELATIONSHIP TO SERVICE OPERATIONS

#

# =============================================================================

Service Operations owns service-specific recovery.

Incident Operations coordinates cross-service and
cross-domain response.

Service health SHALL not alone define incident
closure.

# =============================================================================

#

# 140. RELATIONSHIP TO INFRASTRUCTURE OPERATIONS

#

# =============================================================================

Infrastructure Operations owns recovery of
technical foundations.

Incident Operations coordinates broader impact and
dependency-aware restoration.

Infrastructure recovery SHALL not automatically
declare application recovery.

# =============================================================================

#

# 141. RELATIONSHIP TO DATA OPERATIONS

#

# =============================================================================

Data Operations owns recovery of authoritative data
state.

Incident Operations ensures data incidents preserve

impact assessment,

consumer coordination,

reconciliation,

and recovery validation.

# =============================================================================

#

# 142. RELATIONSHIP TO SECURITY OPERATIONS

#

# =============================================================================

Security Operations owns security-specific

containment,

eradication,

forensics,

and trusted recovery.

Incident Operations provides institutional
coordination where security incidents affect
broader capability.

# =============================================================================

#

# 143. RELATIONSHIP TO TRADING OPERATIONS

#

# =============================================================================

Trading Operations owns financial and trading
recovery.

Incident Operations coordinates cross-domain
response.

Trading resumption SHALL require trading-specific
readiness and reconciliation.

# =============================================================================

#

#

the following SHALL be established where

Recovery Procedure Defined

Dependency Order Known

Authoritative State Identified

Backup Defined Where Required

Restore Procedure Defined

Reconciliation Defined

Validation Defined

Resumption Authority Defined

Communication Path Defined

Evidence Sources Known

Runbooks Available

Emergency Access Defined

Temporary Control Governance Defined

Post-Incident Review Defined

Corrective Action Tracking Defined

No Unowned Critical Alert

Capability failing mandatory criteria SHALL remain
incident-readiness incomplete.

# =============================================================================

#

# 145. MINIMUM INCIDENT RESPONSE GATE

#

# =============================================================================

For every material active incident,
the following SHALL be established as soon as
operationally possible.

Incident Identity Assigned

Incident Declared

Severity Assigned

Incident Commander Assigned

Affected Scope Identified

Known Impact Recorded

Unknowns Recorded

Immediate Risk Assessed

Containment Decision Made

Domain Owners Engaged

Communication Established

Timeline Started

Major Actions Tracked

Evidence Preserved

Recovery Strategy Defined

Resumption Criteria Defined

Failure of a mandatory condition SHALL be corrected
during active response.

# =============================================================================

#

# 146. MINIMUM RECOVERY GATE

#

# =============================================================================

Before material capability is declared recovered,
the following SHALL be established where
applicable.

Incident Contained

Trusted Target State Defined

Authoritative State Identified

Dependencies Restored

Security Validated

Configuration Validated

Data Validated

Service Behaviour Validated

External State Reconciled

Financial State Reconciled Where Applicable

Unknown Critical State Resolved

Monitoring Restored

Residual Risk Understood

Temporary Controls Documented

Domain Readiness Confirmed

Resumption Authority Confirmed

Evidence Preserved

Recovery SHALL NOT be declared solely because
technical availability returned.

# =============================================================================

#

# 147. MINIMUM INCIDENT CLOSURE GATE

#

# =============================================================================

Before a material incident is closed,
the following SHALL be established where
applicable.

Active Impact Ended

Recovery Completed

Required Reconciliation Completed

Residual Risk Recorded

Timeline Completed

Major Decisions Recorded

Evidence Preserved

Root Cause or Sufficient Causal Analysis Completed

Contributing Factors Identified

Corrective Actions Defined

Corrective Action Owners Assigned

Runbook Updates Identified

Monitoring Improvements Identified

Documentation Updates Identified

Institutional Learning Preserved

Incident SHALL remain open or follow-up SHALL remain
explicit if mandatory work is incomplete.

# =============================================================================

#

# 148. INCIDENT VALIDATION QUESTIONS

#

# =============================================================================

Every critical capability shall answer the
following institutional incident and recovery
questions.

How is failure detected?

Who receives the alert?

Who owns the incident?

Who may declare the incident?

How is severity determined?

What is the blast radius?

What must be contained first?

What is the safe state?

What emergency controls exist?

What dependencies must recover first?

What is the authoritative state?

What data may be lost?

What is the RPO?

What is the RTO?

Where are backups?

Have backups been restored successfully?

How is state reconciled?

How is security validated?

How is domain correctness validated?

Who may authorize resumption?

Can operation resume in restricted mode?

What unknown state would block resumption?

What evidence must be preserved?

How is the incident timeline maintained?

How are emergency changes tracked?

How are temporary controls removed?

How is root cause determined?

How are corrective actions tracked?

How is recurrence detected?

Can the complete incident be reconstructed?

Can the capability recover without relying on one
individual's memory?

If any mandatory answer is negative,

incident and recovery readiness remains incomplete.
