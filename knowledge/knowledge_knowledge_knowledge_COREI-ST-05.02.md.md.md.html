COREI STANDARDS PRINCIPLES

STANDARDS PRINCIPLES

VERSION

STATUS

APPROVED

CLASSIFICATION

CONSTITUTIONAL

1. PURPOSE

The COREI Standards Principles establish the
constitutional principles governing every
institutional standard within the COREI
Institution.

These principles define the permanent rules that
preserve consistency, interoperability, quality,
traceability, and institutional continuity across
all engineering, platforms, systems, repositories,
operations, and future institutional capabilities.

No institutional standard shall violate these
principles.

2. CONSTITUTIONAL PRINCIPLE

Standards exist to preserve the constitutional
consistency of the COREI Institution.

Every institutional standard shall faithfully
translate constitutional Identity, Governance,
Architecture, and Engineering into consistent,
repeatable, measurable institutional practice.

Standards preserve institutional integrity across
all implementations.

3. FUNDAMENTAL STANDARDS PRINCIPLES

The COREI Institution follows the following
constitutional standards principles.

Principle 1

Identity Before Standardization.

Every standard shall preserve the constitutional
identity of COREI.

Principle 2

Governance Before Compliance.

Every standard shall inherit from constitutional
governance.

Principle 3

Architecture Before Convention.

Standards shall preserve approved architecture.

Standards shall never redefine architecture.

Principle 4

Engineering Before Specification.

Standards govern engineering.

Specifications implement standards.

Principle 5

Institution Before Technology.

Standards shall remain institution-centric.

Technology-specific standards shall inherit from
institutional standards.

Principle 6

Consistency Before Convenience.

Institutional consistency shall always take
precedence over local optimization.

Principle 7

Clarity Before Complexity.

Every standard shall remain explicit,
understandable,
traceable,
and testable.

Principle 8

Compliance Before Adoption.

No implementation shall be approved without
demonstrated compliance with applicable standards.

Principle 9

Knowledge Preservation.

Every standard shall preserve institutional
knowledge and engineering maturity.

Principle 10

Continuous Improvement.

Standards shall continuously evolve through
validated institutional learning while preserving
constitutional continuity.

4. STANDARDS PRIORITIES

When standards conflict, the following
constitutional priority order shall apply.

Identity

↓

Governance

↓

Architecture

↓

Engineering

↓

Institutional Standards

↓

Platform Standards

↓

Domain Standards

↓

Implementation Standards

↓

Specifications

↓

Technology Preferences

Lower priorities shall never compromise higher
constitutional priorities.

5. STANDARDS CONSTRAINTS

Institutional standards shall never

contradict constitutional Identity,

violate Governance,

modify approved Architecture,

create engineering ambiguity,

introduce inconsistent implementations,

encourage undocumented exceptions,

permit uncontrolled divergence,

or compromise institutional continuity.

Standards shall strengthen institutional
consistency rather than individual preferences.

6. STANDARDS APPLICATION

These principles govern every institutional
standard, including

engineering standards,

repository standards,

platform standards,

security standards,

data standards,

documentation standards,

coding standards,

testing standards,

deployment standards,

operational standards,

AI standards,

and every future institutional standard.

All standards shall inherit these constitutional
principles.

7. STANDARDS VALIDATION

Every institutional standard shall answer the
following constitutional questions.

Does it preserve COREI Identity?

Does it comply with Governance?

Does it preserve Architecture?

Does it strengthen Engineering?

Is it clear and measurable?

Can compliance be validated?

Will it improve institutional consistency?

Will it preserve institutional knowledge?

Will future generations benefit?

If any answer is negative,

the standard shall be reviewed before approval.

8. STANDARDS DECLARATION

COREI Standards exist to preserve one
constitutional institution across unlimited
implementations.

Every institutional standard shall strengthen

constitutional consistency,

engineering quality,

architectural fidelity,

operational reliability,

knowledge preservation,

institutional interoperability,

and long-term institutional continuity.

Standards shall remain stable while enabling
continuous institutional evolution.

9. AUTHORITY

This document defines the constitutional
Standards Principles of COREI.

Every institutional standard,

engineering standard,

repository standard,

platform standard,

security standard,

data standard,

documentation standard,

implementation guide,

specification,

and future institutional standard

shall comply with these constitutional principles.

Changes to these principles require constitutional
review.
