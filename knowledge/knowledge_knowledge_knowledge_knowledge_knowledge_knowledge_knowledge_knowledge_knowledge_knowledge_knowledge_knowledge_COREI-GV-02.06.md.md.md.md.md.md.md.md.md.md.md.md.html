COREI ACCOUNTABILITY

DOCUMENT ID

COREI-GV-02.06

DOCUMENT NAME

ACCOUNTABILITY

VERSION

1.0

STATUS

APPROVED

CLASSIFICATION

CONSTITUTIONAL

1. PURPOSE

The COREI Accountability Model defines the
constitutional ownership of institutional
responsibilities, decisions, outcomes, and
continuous learning.

Its purpose is to ensure that every institutional
capability has clear ownership, explicit
responsibility, and measurable accountability.

Institutional accountability protects the
long-term integrity of COREI.

2. ACCOUNTABILITY PHILOSOPHY

Within COREI,

accountability is not blame.

Accountability is constitutional ownership of
institutional responsibility.

Authority grants the right to decide.

Accountability accepts responsibility for the
decision and its outcomes.

Accountability strengthens institutional trust.

3. ACCOUNTABILITY PRINCIPLES

Every institutional capability shall have

one accountable owner,

clearly defined responsibilities,

measurable outcomes,

transparent ownership,

continuous review,

and institutional traceability.

Accountability shall always be explicit.

Institutional ambiguity is prohibited.

4. ACCOUNTABILITY HIERARCHY

Institutional accountability follows the
constitutional hierarchy.

Identity

↓

Governance

↓

Authority

↓

Responsibility

↓

Decision

↓

Execution

↓

Outcome

↓

Learning

↓

Institutional Improvement

Each level strengthens accountability
for the next.

Accountability exists throughout the
entire institutional lifecycle.

5. ACCOUNTABILITY RESPONSIBILITIES

Every accountable owner is responsible for

constitutional compliance,

decision quality,

execution quality,

resource stewardship,

risk awareness,

continuous improvement,

institutional learning,

and preservation of long-term capability.

Accountability extends beyond task completion.

It includes institutional outcomes.

6. ACCOUNTABILITY MODEL

Every institutional responsibility shall define

Accountable Owner

Responsible Participants

Decision Authority

Scope

Expected Outcomes

Success Criteria

Measurement Criteria

Review Frequency

Improvement Actions

Institutional accountability shall remain
complete and auditable.

7. ACCOUNTABILITY VALIDATION

Every significant institutional activity
shall answer the following questions.

Who owns this capability?

Who approves decisions?

Who executes the work?

Who reviews outcomes?

Who captures institutional learning?

How is success measured?

How will improvements be implemented?

If ownership cannot be identified,

the capability shall not proceed.

8. ACCOUNTABILITY CONTINUITY

Institutional accountability shall survive

organizational restructuring,

leadership succession,

technology modernization,

engineering evolution,

platform replacement,

automation,

and institutional growth.

Accountability belongs to institutional
responsibilities,

not individuals.

Individuals temporarily fulfill
institutional accountability.

9. ACCOUNTABILITY DECLARATION

COREI maintains institutional accountability
through explicit ownership,
clear responsibilities,
transparent governance,
continuous review,
and disciplined institutional learning.

Every institutional capability shall have
one accountable owner,
one defined purpose,
one measurable outcome,
and one continuous improvement path.

Institutional accountability exists to strengthen
trust, continuity, and long-term institutional
mastery.

10. AUTHORITY

This document defines the constitutional
Accountability Model of COREI.

Every Governance Framework,

Decision Process,

Authority Model,

Architecture,

Engineering Practice,

Operational Capability,

Artificial Intelligence System,

Research Initiative,

Knowledge Asset,

and future institutional capability

shall comply with this Accountability Model.

Changes to this document require
constitutional review.
