# =============================================================================

#

# 1. PURPOSE

#

# =============================================================================

The COREI Continuity and Resilience Operations
document defines the authoritative institutional
model for preserving critical capability through

failure,

degradation,

disruption,

dependency loss,

provider failure,

infrastructure failure,

security events,

market disruption,

regional disruption,

and organizational transition.

Its purpose is to ensure that COREI can

absorb disruption,

preserve essential authority,

maintain safe operating states,

continue critical functions,

recover trusted capability,

and evolve without losing institutional continuity.

Resilience SHALL be engineered.

Continuity SHALL be operationally executable.

# =============================================================================

#

# 2. SCOPE

#

# =============================================================================

This document governs continuity and resilience
across

infrastructure,

networks,

storage,

databases,

messaging,

platforms,

services,

applications,

data,

security,

identity,

providers,

brokers,

trading,

risk,

portfolio,

execution,

positions,

performance,

automation,

artificial intelligence systems,

operator environments,

knowledge,

and institutional authority.

It governs

criticality,

dependency analysis,

failure domains,

redundancy,

degradation,

fallback,

failover,

recovery,

business continuity,

disaster recovery,

operational continuity,

knowledge continuity,

and resilience validation.

# =============================================================================

#

# 3. CONTINUITY PRINCIPLE

#

# =============================================================================

Continuity Operations SHALL preserve the chain

Identify Critical Capability

↓

Identify Dependencies

↓

Identify Failure Modes

↓

Define Minimum Safe State

↓

Define Continuity Strategy

↓

Implement Controls

↓

Validate Controls

↓

Operate

↓

Detect Disruption

↓

Degrade Safely

↓

Continue or Recover

↓

Reconcile

↓

Restore Normal State

↓

Learn

Continuity SHALL be designed before disruption.

# =============================================================================

#

# 4. RESILIENCE PRINCIPLE

#

# =============================================================================

Resilience is the ability of COREI capability to

withstand,

absorb,

adapt to,

recover from,

and learn from

disruption.

Resilience SHALL include more than redundancy.

It SHALL include

authority,

state,

data,

people,

process,

technology,

and recovery.

# =============================================================================

#

# 5. CONTINUITY IS NOT AVAILABILITY ALONE

#

# =============================================================================

A system MAY be available while institutionally
unusable.

Continuity SHALL consider

correctness,

security,

data integrity,

financial integrity,

authority,

dependencies,

and safe operation.

Availability alone SHALL NOT define continuity.

# =============================================================================

#

# 6. RESILIENCE IS NOT RESTARTABILITY

#

# =============================================================================

A system that can restart is not necessarily
resilient.

Resilience SHALL consider

state recovery,

dependency recovery,

data recovery,

security recovery,

operational authority,

and domain validation.

# =============================================================================

#

# 7. CONTINUITY OWNERSHIP

#

# =============================================================================

Every critical capability SHALL have explicit
continuity ownership.

Ownership SHALL include responsibility for

continuity design,

failure analysis,

runbooks,

testing,

recovery,

and improvement.

Critical continuity SHALL not be ownerless.

# =============================================================================

#

# 8. CONTINUITY AUTHORITY

#

# =============================================================================

Continuity authority SHALL define who may

declare continuity mode,

activate fallback,

invoke failover,

enter degraded operation,

suspend unsafe capability,

activate disaster recovery,

and authorize normal restoration.

Technical access SHALL NOT automatically create
continuity authority.

# =============================================================================

#

# 9. CRITICAL CAPABILITY

#

# =============================================================================

Critical capability is capability whose failure
creates unacceptable impact to

institutional operation,

security,

capital,

financial integrity,

data integrity,

trading,

risk,

or recovery.

Criticality SHALL be explicit.

# =============================================================================

#

# 10. CRITICALITY CLASSIFICATION

#

# =============================================================================

Capabilities SHOULD be classified according to
institutional criticality.

Classification MAY include

Tier 0 — Existential

Tier 1 — Critical

Tier 2 — Important

Tier 3 — Standard

The exact classification SHALL reflect impact.

# =============================================================================

#

# 11. TIER 0 CAPABILITY

#

# =============================================================================

Tier 0 capability MAY include systems whose failure
threatens

institutional control,

security authority,

financial authority,

or the ability to recover the wider platform.

Tier 0 SHALL receive the strongest continuity
requirements.

# =============================================================================

#

# 12. TIER 1 CAPABILITY

#

# =============================================================================

Tier 1 capability MAY include systems required for

critical production,

trading,

risk,

data,

or operational control.

Tier 1 SHALL maintain validated continuity and
recovery capability.

# =============================================================================

#

# 13. TIER 2 CAPABILITY

#

# =============================================================================

Tier 2 capability MAY support important operation
without being immediately existential.

Continuity controls SHALL reflect business impact.

# =============================================================================

#

# 14. TIER 3 CAPABILITY

#

# =============================================================================

Tier 3 capability MAY tolerate longer disruption.

Lower criticality SHALL not eliminate ownership or
recovery requirements.

# =============================================================================

#

# 15. BUSINESS IMPACT ANALYSIS

#

# =============================================================================

Critical capability SHALL undergo appropriate
business impact analysis.

Analysis SHOULD determine

impact of failure,

maximum tolerable disruption,

dependency impact,

financial impact,

security impact,

data impact,

operational impact,

and recovery priority.

# =============================================================================

#

# 16. MAXIMUM TOLERABLE DOWNTIME

#

# =============================================================================

Critical capability SHOULD define maximum tolerable
downtime where applicable.

Maximum tolerable downtime SHALL reflect
institutional impact.

It SHALL not be confused with target recovery time.

# =============================================================================

#

# 17. RECOVERY TIME OBJECTIVE

#

# =============================================================================

Critical capability SHOULD define RTO where
appropriate.

RTO SHALL represent the target time for restoring
required capability.

RTO SHALL be supported by architecture and tested
procedure.

# =============================================================================

#

# 18. RECOVERY POINT OBJECTIVE

#

# =============================================================================

Critical stateful capability SHOULD define RPO
where appropriate.

RPO SHALL represent acceptable data loss or state
loss.

RPO SHALL be supported by actual data protection
mechanisms.

# =============================================================================

#

# 19. SERVICE LEVEL OBJECTIVE

#

# =============================================================================

Service Level Objectives MAY define normal
operational expectations.

SLO SHALL remain distinct from

RTO,

RPO,

and maximum tolerable downtime.

Normal reliability and disaster recovery are
related but different concerns.

# =============================================================================

#

# 20. DEPENDENCY MAPPING

#

# =============================================================================

Every critical capability SHALL understand its
dependencies.

Dependencies MAY include

compute,

network,

storage,

database,

messaging,

identity,

security,

platforms,

services,

data,

providers,

brokers,

people,

and external authority.

Unknown critical dependency is continuity risk.

# =============================================================================

#

# 21. UPSTREAM DEPENDENCY

#

# =============================================================================

Upstream dependencies SHALL be identified.

A capability SHALL not claim independent resilience
while relying upon a single fragile upstream
dependency.

# =============================================================================

#

# 22. DOWNSTREAM DEPENDENCY

#

# =============================================================================

Critical capability SHALL understand important
downstream consumers.

Recovery order SHALL consider downstream
consequences.

# =============================================================================

#

# 23. TRANSITIVE DEPENDENCY

#

# =============================================================================

Continuity analysis SHALL consider transitive
dependencies.

A redundant service MAY remain non-resilient when
all instances depend upon one shared failure point.

# =============================================================================

#

# 24. SINGLE POINT OF FAILURE

#

# =============================================================================

Critical single points of failure SHALL be

identified,

owned,

risk-assessed,

and corrected or explicitly accepted.

Hidden single points of failure are prohibited.

# =============================================================================

#

# 25. FAILURE DOMAIN

#

# =============================================================================

Failure domains SHALL be explicit.

Failure domains MAY include

process,

host,

node,

rack,

cluster,

availability zone,

region,

provider,

broker,

network,

storage system,

identity system,

or human authority.

Redundancy within one failure domain SHALL not be
misrepresented as independent resilience.

# =============================================================================

#

# 26. CORRELATED FAILURE

#

# =============================================================================

Continuity design SHALL consider correlated
failure.

Examples include

shared credentials,

shared network,

shared storage,

shared provider,

shared configuration,

shared control plane,

and shared human dependency.

Nominal duplication SHALL not hide common failure.

# =============================================================================

#

# 27. REDUNDANCY

#

# =============================================================================

Redundancy MAY improve resilience.

Redundancy SHALL be

purposeful,

independent where required,

observable,

maintained,

and tested.

Unused or untested redundancy is not proven
resilience.

# =============================================================================

#

# 28. ACTIVE-ACTIVE

#

# =============================================================================

Active-active designs MAY provide continuity where

state consistency,

traffic distribution,

failure handling,

and split-brain prevention

are understood.

Active-active SHALL not create duplicate
consequential action.

# =============================================================================

#

# 29. ACTIVE-PASSIVE

#

# =============================================================================

Active-passive designs MAY provide controlled
failover.

Passive capability SHALL maintain sufficient

readiness,

configuration,

data,

security,

and capacity

to meet continuity objectives.

# =============================================================================

#

# 30. HOT STANDBY

#

# =============================================================================

Hot standby capability SHALL remain continuously or
near-continuously ready according to design.

Readiness SHALL be validated.

# =============================================================================

#

# 31. WARM STANDBY

#

# =============================================================================

Warm standby capability SHALL define

activation steps,

data synchronization,

configuration synchronization,

and expected recovery time.

Warm SHALL not be assumed immediately ready.

# =============================================================================

#

# 32. COLD STANDBY

#

# =============================================================================

Cold standby capability SHALL define

provisioning,

configuration,

data restoration,

security initialization,

and validation.

Cold recovery SHALL be tested against actual RTO.

# =============================================================================

#

# 33. FAILOVER

#

# =============================================================================

Failover SHALL transfer operation to alternate
capability.

Failover SHALL validate

target readiness,

state consistency,

identity,

networking,

dependencies,

authority,

and downstream impact.

Failover SHALL not create split-brain.

# =============================================================================

#

# 34. AUTOMATIC FAILOVER

#

# =============================================================================

Automatic failover MAY be used where failure
detection and transition are reliable.

Automatic failover SHALL define

trigger,

quorum,

fencing,

state,

authority,

and failure behaviour.

Automatic transition SHALL not create duplicate
financial action.

# =============================================================================

#

# 35. MANUAL FAILOVER

#

# =============================================================================

Manual failover MAY be appropriate where

state uncertainty,

financial consequence,

or complex authority

requires human decision.

Manual failover SHALL have clear runbooks.

# =============================================================================

#

# 36. FAILBACK

#

# =============================================================================

Failback SHALL be treated as a controlled change.

Failback SHALL validate

source readiness,

state synchronization,

traffic movement,

and rollback.

Return to original infrastructure SHALL not be
assumed safe.

# =============================================================================

#

# 37. FENCING

#

# =============================================================================

Stateful and consequential systems SHALL use
appropriate fencing where dual-active operation
could create corruption or duplicate action.

Fencing SHALL prevent the failed or isolated
authority from continuing unsafe operation.

# =============================================================================

#

# 38. SPLIT-BRAIN

#

# =============================================================================

Systems susceptible to split-brain SHALL define
prevention and recovery.

Split-brain risk SHALL be considered in

databases,

clusters,

messaging,

control planes,

and trading execution.

Unknown authority SHALL not be silently accepted.

# =============================================================================

#

# 39. QUORUM

#

# =============================================================================

Where quorum is used,
quorum requirements SHALL be understood.

Loss of quorum SHALL trigger defined safe
behaviour.

Availability SHALL not override consistency where
consistency is mandatory.

# =============================================================================

#

# 40. DEGRADATION

#

# =============================================================================

Critical capability SHOULD define degraded
operating modes.

Degradation SHALL preserve essential safety and
authority.

Degraded operation SHALL be visible.

# =============================================================================

#

# 41. GRACEFUL DEGRADATION

#

# =============================================================================

Graceful degradation SHALL reduce non-essential
capability while preserving critical function.

Examples MAY include

read-only operation,

reduced data scope,

restricted strategy operation,

lower throughput,

or delayed non-critical processing.

# =============================================================================

#

# 42. FAIL-CLOSED

#

# =============================================================================

Capability SHALL fail closed where continued action
without required authority or control creates
unacceptable risk.

Examples MAY include

risk approval,

privileged access,

or live execution authorization.

# =============================================================================

#

# 43. FAIL-OPEN

#

# =============================================================================

Fail-open behaviour MAY be used only where
continuity risk is greater than control risk and
explicitly approved.

Fail-open SHALL never be an accidental default.

# =============================================================================

#

# 44. SAFE STATE

#

# =============================================================================

Critical capability SHALL define a safe state.

Safe state SHALL preserve

security,

capital,

data,

state,

and recoverability

over normal throughput or convenience.

# =============================================================================

#

# 45. MINIMUM VIABLE OPERATING STATE

#

# =============================================================================

Critical domains SHOULD define a Minimum Viable
Operating State.

The state SHALL identify

essential capability,

essential dependencies,

essential data,

essential authority,

essential controls,

and prohibited actions.

# =============================================================================

#

# 46. CONTINUITY MODE

#

# =============================================================================

COREI MAY enter explicit continuity mode during
material disruption.

Continuity mode SHALL define

scope,

authority,

operating restrictions,

fallbacks,

temporary controls,

and exit criteria.

# =============================================================================

#

# 47. RESTRICTED CONTINUITY MODE

#

# =============================================================================

Restricted continuity mode SHALL permit only
approved essential activity.

Restrictions MAY apply to

services,

users,

accounts,

brokers,

strategies,

markets,

or regions.

# =============================================================================

#

# 48. READ-ONLY CONTINUITY

#

# =============================================================================

Read-only operation MAY preserve observation while
blocking unsafe mutation.

Read-only state SHALL be explicit.

# =============================================================================

#

# 49. CLOSE-ONLY CONTINUITY

#

# =============================================================================

Trading systems MAY enter close-only mode to permit
risk reduction while blocking new exposure.

Close-only semantics SHALL be explicit.

# =============================================================================

#

# 50. SUSPENDED STATE

#

# =============================================================================

Capability SHALL be suspended when safe continuity
cannot be maintained.

Suspension SHALL be preferable to uncontrolled
operation.

# =============================================================================

#

# 51. FALLBACK

#

# =============================================================================

Fallback capability SHALL be explicitly defined.

Fallback MAY involve

alternate provider,

alternate broker,

alternate data source,

alternate service,

alternate infrastructure,

or manual controlled process.

Fallback SHALL not silently change authority.

# =============================================================================

#

# 52. FALLBACK QUALITY

#

# =============================================================================

Fallback SHALL be evaluated for

capability,

quality,

latency,

coverage,

security,

cost,

and operational limitations.

Fallback SHALL not be assumed equivalent to primary
capability.

# =============================================================================

#

# 53. PROVIDER RESILIENCE

#

# =============================================================================

Critical provider dependency SHALL define

primary provider,

alternate provider where required,

failure detection,

switching policy,

data consistency,

and recovery.

Multiple providers sharing one upstream source MAY
not provide true independence.

# =============================================================================

#

# 54. BROKER RESILIENCE

#

# =============================================================================

Multi-broker capability MAY improve continuity.

Broker resilience SHALL preserve

account boundaries,

risk,

positions,

instrument availability,

and execution semantics.

Broker failure SHALL not trigger unsafe blind
rerouting.

# =============================================================================

#

# 55. MARKET DATA RESILIENCE

#

# =============================================================================

Market data continuity SHALL consider

provider failure,

staleness,

gaps,

quality degradation,

symbol mapping,

and source disagreement.

Fallback data SHALL be validated before
consequential use.

# =============================================================================

#

# 56. DATABASE RESILIENCE

#

# =============================================================================

Database resilience SHALL consider

replication,

backup,

failover,

consistency,

recovery,

and corruption.

Replication SHALL not replace backup.

# =============================================================================

#

# 57. STORAGE RESILIENCE

#

# =============================================================================

Storage resilience SHALL consider

device failure,

node failure,

corruption,

capacity,

backup,

and restoration.

Redundant storage SHALL not eliminate recovery
requirements.

# =============================================================================

#

# 58. MESSAGING RESILIENCE

#

# =============================================================================

Messaging resilience SHALL consider

broker failure,

partition availability,

replication,

ordering,

offsets,

replay,

and duplicate processing.

Recovery SHALL preserve event semantics.

# =============================================================================

#

# 59. NETWORK RESILIENCE

#

# =============================================================================

Network resilience SHALL consider

path failure,

DNS,

routing,

load balancing,

firewalls,

certificates,

and external connectivity.

Network redundancy SHALL avoid shared hidden
dependencies.

# =============================================================================

#

# 60. IDENTITY RESILIENCE

#

# =============================================================================

Identity capability SHALL maintain continuity
without weakening security.

Emergency access SHALL be

controlled,

limited,

auditable,

and recoverable.

Identity outage SHALL not justify uncontrolled
privilege.

# =============================================================================

#

# 61. SECRET AND KEY RESILIENCE

#

# =============================================================================

Critical secrets,
keys,
and certificates

SHALL have appropriate

backup,

rotation,

recovery,

and access controls.

Recovery SHALL preserve confidentiality.

# =============================================================================

#

# 62. OBSERVABILITY RESILIENCE

#

# =============================================================================

Critical operations SHOULD retain minimum
observability during disruption.

Monitoring failure SHALL not leave operators blind
to critical state.

Observability itself SHALL have continuity
consideration.

# =============================================================================

#

# 63. CONTROL PLANE RESILIENCE

#

# =============================================================================

Critical control planes SHALL be resilient.

Loss of control plane SHALL not automatically
destroy data-plane safety.

Emergency operational procedures SHALL be defined.

# =============================================================================

#

# 64. DATA PLANE RESILIENCE

#

# =============================================================================

Data-plane capability SHALL define behaviour when
control-plane capability is degraded.

Autonomous continued operation SHALL remain bounded
by existing authority.

# =============================================================================

#

# 65. PLATFORM RESILIENCE

#

# =============================================================================

Shared platforms SHALL define continuity according
to downstream criticality.

Platform resilience SHALL not hide consumer-specific
readiness requirements.

# =============================================================================

#

# 66. SERVICE RESILIENCE

#

# =============================================================================

Services SHALL define behaviour under

dependency failure,

timeout,

partial failure,

retry,

overload,

and recovery.

Service resilience SHALL prevent cascading failure.

# =============================================================================

#

# 67. TIMEOUTS

#

# =============================================================================

Distributed operations SHALL use bounded timeouts.

Unbounded waiting SHALL not consume resources
indefinitely.

# =============================================================================

#

# 68. RETRIES

#

# =============================================================================

Retries SHALL be

bounded,

observable,

and appropriate to operation semantics.

Retries SHALL not amplify outages or duplicate
consequential actions.

# =============================================================================

#

# 69. BACKOFF

#

# =============================================================================

Retry behaviour SHOULD use appropriate backoff.

Backoff SHALL reduce synchronized retry storms.

# =============================================================================

#

# 70. JITTER

#

# =============================================================================

Jitter MAY be used to reduce synchronized retry
behaviour.

Retry timing SHALL remain bounded.

# =============================================================================

#

# 71. CIRCUIT BREAKERS

#

# =============================================================================

Circuit breakers MAY prevent repeated calls to
failing dependencies.

Circuit state SHALL be observable.

Recovery probing SHALL be controlled.

# =============================================================================

#

# 72. BULKHEADS

#

# =============================================================================

Bulkhead isolation MAY limit failure propagation.

Resources SHOULD be partitioned where one workload
could otherwise exhaust shared critical capacity.

# =============================================================================

#

# 73. LOAD SHEDDING

#

# =============================================================================

Systems MAY shed non-critical load to preserve
critical capability.

Load-shedding policy SHALL define priority.

# =============================================================================

#

# 74. BACKPRESSURE

#

# =============================================================================

Streaming and event-driven systems SHALL support
appropriate backpressure.

Unbounded accumulation SHALL not silently exhaust
system capacity.

# =============================================================================

#

# 75. QUEUE RESILIENCE

#

# =============================================================================

Queues SHALL define

capacity,

retention,

overflow,

retry,

dead-letter handling,

and recovery.

Queue growth SHALL be observable.

# =============================================================================

#

# 76. CACHING RESILIENCE

#

# =============================================================================

Cache failure SHALL not create hidden correctness
failure.

Systems SHALL define whether cache is

optional,

authoritative,

or reconstructable.

Cache recovery SHALL preserve semantics.

# =============================================================================

#

# 77. CAPACITY RESILIENCE

#

# =============================================================================

Critical capability SHALL maintain capacity
appropriate to

normal load,

expected peak,

failure conditions,

and recovery.

Failover capacity SHALL be considered.

# =============================================================================

#

# 78. HEADROOM

#

# =============================================================================

Critical systems SHOULD maintain operational
headroom.

Operating continuously at maximum capacity reduces
resilience.

# =============================================================================

#

# 79. OVERLOAD PROTECTION

#

# =============================================================================

Systems SHALL protect themselves from uncontrolled
overload.

Protection MAY include

rate limits,

queues,

load shedding,

admission control,

or scaling.

# =============================================================================

#

# 80. CASCADING FAILURE

#

# =============================================================================

Continuity design SHALL consider cascading failure.

One dependency failure SHALL not unnecessarily
collapse unrelated capability.

# =============================================================================

#

# 81. THUNDERING HERD

#

# =============================================================================

Recovery and retry mechanisms SHALL avoid
thundering-herd behaviour.

Mass simultaneous reconnection or retry SHALL be
controlled.

# =============================================================================

#

# 82. RECOVERY STORM

#

# =============================================================================

Large-scale recovery SHALL avoid overwhelming

databases,

providers,

brokers,

networks,

or dependent services.

Recovery sequencing SHALL consider capacity.

# =============================================================================

#

# 83. DATA CONTINUITY

#

# =============================================================================

Critical data SHALL preserve

availability,

integrity,

lineage,

recoverability,

and authority.

Data continuity SHALL include both storage and
consumer recovery.

# =============================================================================

#

# 84. BACKUP

#

# =============================================================================

Backup SHALL protect against loss and corruption.

Backup SHALL define

scope,

frequency,

retention,

security,

integrity,

and restoration.

Backup without restore validation is incomplete.

# =============================================================================

#

# 85. REPLICATION

#

# =============================================================================

Replication MAY improve availability and reduce
data loss.

Replication SHALL not be treated as protection
against every form of corruption or deletion.

# =============================================================================

#

# 86. IMMUTABLE BACKUP

#

# =============================================================================

Critical capability MAY require immutable or
logically isolated backup.

Backup protection SHALL consider

accidental deletion,

malicious deletion,

and security compromise.

# =============================================================================

#

# 87. RESTORE

#

# =============================================================================

Restore procedures SHALL be

documented,

tested,

and validated.

Restore success SHALL include domain correctness,
not only file or database availability.

# =============================================================================

#

# 88. POINT-IN-TIME RECOVERY

#

# =============================================================================

Where supported,
point-in-time recovery SHALL define

available recovery window,

time precision,

dependencies,

and validation.

Recovery time SHALL be chosen according to
authoritative evidence.

# =============================================================================

#

# 89. DATA RECONSTRUCTION

#

# =============================================================================

Some state MAY be reconstructed from

event logs,

external authorities,

or deterministic recomputation.

Reconstruction procedures SHALL preserve lineage
and correctness.

# =============================================================================

#

# 90. KNOWLEDGE CONTINUITY

#

# =============================================================================

Institutional knowledge SHALL survive

personnel change,

tool change,

technology change,

and organizational growth.

Critical knowledge SHALL not exist only in human
memory.

# =============================================================================

#

# 91. DOCUMENTATION CONTINUITY

#

# =============================================================================

Authoritative documentation SHALL remain

versioned,

discoverable,

maintained,

and connected to implementation.

Outdated documentation SHALL be corrected or
deprecated.

# =============================================================================

#

# 92. OPERATOR CONTINUITY

#

# =============================================================================

Critical operations SHALL not depend upon one
operator.

Capability SHALL support

handover,

training,

runbooks,

access,

# 93. AUTHORITY CONTINUITY

#

Critical institutional authority SHALL define
continuity during

absence,

handover,

incident,

or disaster.

Authority continuity SHALL not create uncontrolled
delegation.

# =============================================================================

#

# 94. SUCCESSION OF RESPONSIBILITY

#

# =============================================================================

Critical operational roles SHOULD define succession
or backup responsibility.

Succession SHALL preserve

scope,

authority,

and accountability.

# =============================================================================

#

# 95. TEAM CONTINUITY

#

# =============================================================================

Critical teams SHALL preserve operational knowledge
through

documentation,

handover,

cross-training,

and shared ownership.

Single-person dependency is continuity risk.

# =============================================================================

#

# 96. VENDOR CONTINUITY

#

# =============================================================================

Critical vendor dependency SHALL be assessed for

failure,

service withdrawal,

commercial change,

technical change,

and replacement difficulty.

Vendor lock-in SHALL be understood.

# =============================================================================

#

# 97. TECHNOLOGY CONTINUITY

#

# =============================================================================

COREI SHALL preserve institutional capability
through technology evolution.

Technology replacement SHALL preserve

contracts,

data,

authority,

operational behaviour,

and migration evidence.

# =============================================================================

#

# 98. VERSION CONTINUITY

#

# =============================================================================

Critical capability SHALL manage

version compatibility,

upgrade path,

rollback,

and deprecation.

Unsupported versions SHALL not remain hidden
dependencies.

# =============================================================================

#

# 99. SCHEMA CONTINUITY

#

# =============================================================================

Data and event schema evolution SHALL preserve
compatibility according to explicit policy.

Schema change SHALL not silently break recovery or
replay.

# =============================================================================

#

# 100. API CONTINUITY

#

# =============================================================================

Critical API evolution SHALL preserve consumer
continuity.

Breaking changes SHALL be

versioned,

communicated,

and migrated.

# =============================================================================

#

# 101. PROVIDER EXIT CONTINUITY

#

# =============================================================================

Critical provider integrations SHOULD define an
exit strategy where replacement may become
necessary.

Exit planning SHALL consider

data,

contracts,

configuration,

credentials,

migration,

and validation.

# 102. BROKER EXIT CONTINUITY

#

# =============================================================================

Broker dependency SHALL preserve the ability to

reconcile,

close,

transfer,

or migrate

according to operational and legal constraints.

Broker exit SHALL not create unknown financial
state.

# =============================================================================

#

# 103. REGIONAL CONTINUITY

#

# =============================================================================

Regional continuity SHALL consider

infrastructure,

network,

provider,

broker,

market,

regulatory,

and operational dependencies.

Multi-region architecture SHALL not be assumed
necessary for every capability.

Criticality SHALL determine design.

# =============================================================================

#

# 104. JURISDICTIONAL CONTINUITY

#

# =============================================================================

Institutional capability spanning jurisdictions
SHALL understand continuity constraints arising
from

regulation,

market access,

data location,

broker availability,

and operational authority.

# =============================================================================

#

# 105. MARKET CONTINUITY

#

# =============================================================================

Trading continuity SHALL respect market-specific

sessions,

halts,

closures,

liquidity,

and extraordinary conditions.

System continuity SHALL not imply market
availability.

# =============================================================================

#

# 106. 24X7 CONTINUITY

#

# =============================================================================

Continuous operations SHALL define

ownership,

handover,

monitoring,

escalation,

maintenance,

and recovery

across time zones and day boundaries.

24x7 operation SHALL not depend upon permanent
human attention.

# =============================================================================

#

# 107. MAINTENANCE CONTINUITY

#

# =============================================================================

Planned maintenance SHALL preserve required
continuity.

Maintenance strategy MAY include

rolling change,

redundancy,

temporary restriction,

or planned outage.

Maintenance SHALL be treated as controlled
operational risk.

# =============================================================================

#

# 108. CHANGE RESILIENCE

#

# =============================================================================

Critical systems SHALL remain resilient to change.

Change SHALL consider

deployment failure,

configuration failure,

schema incompatibility,

state migration,

and rollback.

Change is a major source of operational disruption.

# =============================================================================

#

# 109. DEPLOYMENT RESILIENCE

#

# =============================================================================

Deployment strategy SHALL preserve appropriate
continuity.

Strategies MAY include

rolling deployment,

blue-green deployment,

canary deployment,

or controlled replacement.

The strategy SHALL match system state and risk.

# =============================================================================

#

# 110. CONFIGURATION RESILIENCE

#

# =============================================================================

Configuration SHALL be

versioned,

validated,

recoverable,

and drift-detectable.

Configuration loss SHALL not prevent recovery.

# =============================================================================

#

# 111. SECRET ROTATION CONTINUITY

#

# =============================================================================

Secret rotation SHALL preserve continuity without
creating uncontrolled overlap.

Old and new credentials SHALL be managed according
to explicit transition policy.

# =============================================================================

#

# 112. CERTIFICATE CONTINUITY

#

# =============================================================================

Certificate lifecycle SHALL prevent avoidable
expiry-driven outages.

Expiry,

renewal,

deployment,

and validation

SHALL be observable.

# =============================================================================

#

# 113. DNS CONTINUITY

#

# =============================================================================

Critical DNS dependency SHALL consider

provider failure,

cache behaviour,

TTL,

propagation,

and recovery.

DNS change SHALL not be treated as instantaneous.

# =============================================================================

#

# 114. TIME CONTINUITY

#

# =============================================================================

Systems dependent upon accurate time SHALL maintain
time synchronization resilience.

Clock drift MAY affect

security,

events,

ordering,

trading,

and reconciliation.

Time health SHALL be observable where critical.

# =============================================================================

#

# 115. OBSERVABILITY DURING FAILURE

#

# =============================================================================

Resilience SHALL include the ability to observe
failure.

Critical failure SHALL not remove all

logs,

metrics,

traces,

alerts,

and operational visibility.

# =============================================================================

#

# 116. OUT-OF-BAND OPERATIONS

#

# =============================================================================

Critical environments MAY require out-of-band
operational capability.

Out-of-band access SHALL remain

secure,

controlled,

tested,

and auditable.

# =============================================================================

#

# 117. BREAK-GLASS CONTINUITY

#

# =============================================================================

Emergency access SHALL support continuity when
normal access paths fail.

Break-glass capability SHALL be

limited,

protected,

monitored,

audited,

and reviewed.

# =============================================================================

#

# 118. RESILIENCE TESTING

#

# =============================================================================

Testing MAY include

component failure,

dependency failure,

provider outage,

broker outage,

network failure,

storage failure,

database failover,

restore,

regional disruption,

or operator unavailability.

# =============================================================================

#

# 119. TABLETOP EXERCISES

#

# =============================================================================

Tabletop exercises MAY validate

authority,

decision making,

communication,

runbooks,

and dependency understanding.

Tabletop success SHALL not replace technical
testing.

# =============================================================================

#

# 120. FAILOVER TESTING

#

# =============================================================================

Failover procedures SHALL be tested according to
criticality.

Testing SHALL validate

detection,

transition,

state,

traffic,

authority,

and recovery.

# =============================================================================

#

# 121. RESTORE TESTING

#

# =============================================================================

Critical backups SHALL be restored periodically.

Restore testing SHALL validate usable recovered
state.

# =============================================================================

#

# 122. DISASTER RECOVERY TESTING

#

# =============================================================================

Critical disaster recovery capability SHALL be
tested.

people,

infrastructure,

data,

security,

applications,

and resumption.

# 123. CHAOS TESTING

# =============================================================================

hypothesis,

scope,

blast radius,

safety controls,

# =============================================================================

actions,

timings,

failures,

results,

# 125. RESILIENCE METRICS

replication health,

backup health,

failover readiness,

dependency health,

#

# =============================================================================

Critical continuity information MAY be exposed

emergency access,

resumption,

fallback,

active failures,

fallbacks,

temporary controls,

recovery state,

risks,

# =============================================================================

reason,

scope,

risk,

owner,

compensating controls,

monitoring,

Permanent undocumented continuity exceptions are
prohibited.

# =============================================================================

#

# 134. NO RESILIENCE THEATRE

#

# =============================================================================

COREI SHALL NOT claim resilience based solely upon

multiple replicas,

multiple servers,

multiple regions,

or backup existence.

Resilience SHALL be supported by

independence,

testing,

recovery,

and evidence.

# =============================================================================

#

# 135. NO BACKUP-ONLY ASSUMPTION

#

# =============================================================================

Backup existence SHALL NOT be treated as proven
recoverability.

Restore SHALL be tested.

# =============================================================================

#

# 136. NO REPLICATION-ONLY ASSUMPTION

#

# =============================================================================

Replication SHALL NOT be treated as protection from

corruption,

deletion,

misconfiguration,

or malicious action

without appropriate additional controls.

# =============================================================================

#

# 137. NO SINGLE-PERSON CONTINUITY

#

# =============================================================================

Critical capability SHALL NOT depend upon one
individual for

operation,

recovery,

authority,

or knowledge

without explicit accepted risk.

# =============================================================================

#

# 138. NO HIDDEN SINGLE POINT OF FAILURE

#

# =============================================================================

Critical single points of failure SHALL not remain
unknown.

Known unavoidable single points SHALL be explicitly
owned and risk-managed.

# =============================================================================

#

# 139. NO BLIND FAILOVER

#

# =============================================================================

Failover SHALL NOT occur without understanding

target readiness,

state,

and duplicate-action risk

where consequential.

# =============================================================================

#

# 140. NO AUTOMATIC TRUST AFTER RECOVERY

#

# =============================================================================

Recovered capability SHALL NOT automatically be
trusted.

Validation and reconciliation SHALL establish
readiness.

# =============================================================================

#

# 141. NO CONTINUITY THROUGH CONTROL BYPASS

#

# =============================================================================

Continuity SHALL NOT be achieved by silently
bypassing

security,

risk,

financial controls,

or institutional authority.

Unsafe continuity is failure.

# =============================================================================

#

# 142. NO PERMANENT EMERGENCY MODE

#

# =============================================================================

Emergency,
fallback,
or degraded modes

SHALL have explicit exit criteria.

Temporary operating modes SHALL not become hidden
normal architecture.

# =============================================================================

#

# 143. NO FALSE MULTI-REGION RESILIENCE

#

# =============================================================================

Multi-region deployment SHALL not be called
resilient when regions share critical hidden
dependencies that can fail together.

# =============================================================================

#

# 144. NO FALSE MULTI-PROVIDER RESILIENCE

#

# =============================================================================

Multiple providers SHALL not be considered
independent when they share the same critical
upstream source or failure domain.

# =============================================================================

#

# 145. NO FALSE RECOVERY OBJECTIVES

#

# =============================================================================

RTO and RPO SHALL not be declared without
supporting

architecture,

procedure,

and validation.

Unproven objectives SHALL be identified as targets,
not capabilities.
